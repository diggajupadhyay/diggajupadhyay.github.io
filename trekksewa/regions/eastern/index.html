Kanchenjunga & Makalu पूर्वी नेपाल

Long, quiet approaches to the third and fifth highest mountains.

Treks
2
Places
36
Best months
Apr–May, Oct–Nov
Highest place listed
5,143 mPangpema

East of Everest the trails become long and lightly travelled. Kanchenjunga, on the border with Sikkim, takes three weeks to visit both base camps. Makalu Base Camp climbs from the hot Arun valley, among the lowest in Nepal, to the foot of an 8,485 m peak.

These are committing routes: lodges are basic or absent in places, evacuation is slow, and walking days are long. They suit trekkers who have already been to altitude and want the mountains without the crowds.

When to go

April to May and October to November. The east gets the monsoon first and longest, so the season windows are shorter than further west.

Getting there

Fly Kathmandu to Bhadrapur for Kanchenjunga or to Tumlingtar for Makalu, then continue by jeep on rough roads to the trailheads.

Permits used in Eastern Nepal

All permits

Kanchenjunga RAP

Restricted area

Kanchenjunga restricted area permit · Department of Immigration

All foreign nationalsUSD 20person per week for the first four weeks, then USD 25 a week

Where: Department of Immigration in Kathmandu, through a registered trekking agency only.

Since 22 March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker; before that the minimum was two. You still have to book through a registered agency and walk with a licensed guide, with at most seven trekkers per guide. Covers Phaktanglung, Mikwakhola and Sirijunga rural municipalities in Taplejung. The conservation area permit is extra.

Agency required

Kanchenjunga CA

Conservation area

Kanchenjunga Conservation Area Permit · Kanchenjunga Conservation Area Management Council

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 500person, single entry (unconfirmed)

Where: Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu, normally arranged by your agency with the restricted-area permit. Foreign trekkers are also registered at check posts on the trail, for example at Ghunsa.

NPR 2,000 for foreigners is consistent across the Nepal Tourism Board and most agencies. The SAARC rate is less certain: most agencies say NPR 500, the tourism board's page says NPR 200. A few agency pages list NPR 3,000, which is the NTNC rate and does not apply here, because this area is run by a local council under the Department of National Parks.

Makalu Barun National Park

National park

Makalu Barun National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person (unconfirmed)

Where: Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu, or the park entry posts at Num and Seduwa on the trail.

Children under 10 are exempt. Foreigner and SAARC rates match the Nepal Tourism Board page; whether VAT is added on top is unconfirmed. Nepali citizens are quoted NPR 100 by a current agency guide, while the board's page still shows NPR 25. Agencies also describe a separate charge or permit for Makalu Rural Municipality but quote it inconsistently, from NPR 2,000 to USD 20 a week, so ask your agency what applies on your route.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.
