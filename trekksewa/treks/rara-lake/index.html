Rara Lake

A ten-day circuit from the Jumla airstrip through the old Khasa capital of Sinja to Rara Lake at 2,990 m, returning over the Ghurchi Lagna and Danphe Lagna passes.

Overview

Rara is the largest lake in Nepal, nearly eleven square kilometres of clear water at 2,990 m, ringed by pine, spruce and juniper inside the country's smallest national park. It lies in Mugu, in the Karnali, one of the least developed corners of Nepal. The walk from Jumla is the classic way to reach it: out through the Sinja valley, where the Khasa kings ruled from the twelfth to the fourteenth century and the Nepali language took shape, and back over two forested passes.

Altitude is modest and the passes need no special skill outside winter. What makes the route demanding is the country itself. Villages are poor, lodges are few and basic, and food beyond rice, lentils and potatoes is whatever you or your crew carry. Many groups still camp, or mix tents with homestays and the simple lodges at the lake.

A road now reaches Jumla and continues, roughly, to Gamgadhi near the lake, and there is an airstrip at Talcha a few hours' walk from the shore, so Rara can also be visited in three or four days. The loop described here is for people who want the walk as much as the lake. Stage names and heights vary a good deal between sources; treat the figures as approximate.

Highlights

  • Rara Lake, clear, cold and nearly empty of visitors
  • The view of the whole lake from Murma Top
  • Sinja, seat of the medieval Khasa kingdom
  • Pine, spruce and birch forest on the Ghurchi Lagna and Danphe Lagna
  • Jumla's high-altitude red rice and apple orchards
  • Village life in the Karnali, far from the main trekking circuits

Moderate: Nothing above about 3,700 m, but long days, three forested passes and very basic food and lodging.

Ways to change it

Fly to Talcha

Flights from Nepalgunj land at the Talcha airstrip near Gamgadhi, two to three hours' walk from the lake, or less if you ride part of the way. With a night or two at the lake the whole visit takes three to five days from Nepalgunj, weather allowing.

The direct trail both ways

Walking in and out by the Danphe Lagna, Bumra and Pina takes about three days each way and leaves out Sinja. It is the quicker option when time is short.

Reverse loop

Going out by the Danphe Lagna and back through Sinja works just as well, and many itineraries run this way.

By road

Buses and jeeps run from Surkhet to Jumla and on towards Gamgadhi in the dry season. Most Nepali visitors arrive this way; it is rough, slow and cheap.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, though checks on the trail are inconsistent. Here a guide is practical as well as official: trails braid between villages without signs, very few people speak English, and food and beds have to be arranged house by house.

Rara National Park

National park

Rara National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person (unconfirmed)

Where: At the park entry posts on the way to the lake, or in advance at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

Foreigner and SAARC rates match the Nepal Tourism Board page. Nepali citizens are most often quoted NPR 100, though the board's page still shows NPR 25. Whether VAT is added to the foreigner fee is unconfirmed. Rara Lake itself needs no restricted-area permit.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.

Getting there

Fly from Kathmandu to Nepalgunj, spend the night, and take a morning flight to Jumla. Flights depend on the weather and are often delayed. Jumla is also on the Karnali Highway from Surkhet, a long and rough journey by bus or jeep that still runs when aircraft cannot.

  • Flight: Kathmandu to Nepalgunj · about 1 hour

    Several flights a day. Buses take ten to twelve hours.

  • Flight: Nepalgunj to Jumla · about 35 min

    Small aircraft in the early morning; cancellations are common in cloud or wind.

  • Bus: Nepalgunj to Jumla · 1–2 days

    By the Karnali Highway through Surkhet and Kalikot: narrow, partly unpaved and tiring, usually with a night on the way.

  • Flight: Nepalgunj to Talcha · about 45 min

    For a short visit: the airstrip is two to three hours' walk from the lake. Less reliable still than the Jumla flight.

Hazards on this route

Unreliable flights

Jumla and Talcha flights are cancelled often, sometimes for days in a row. Keep at least two spare days in your schedule and enough cash to sit out a delay.

Snow on the passes

The Danphe Lagna and Ghurchi Lagna hold snow from December to March and after spring storms, and a buried forest trail is hard to follow. In those months ask in Jumla which trail is open and go with someone local.

Food and stomach trouble

This is a food-deficit region and kitchen standards are basic. Stomach upsets end more Rara treks than altitude does. Treat all water, wash your hands and carry rehydration salts and a few days of reserve food.

Limited medical help

Jumla has a teaching hospital and Gamgadhi a district hospital; between them you rely on your own kit. Helicopter evacuation depends on the weather.

Practical tips

  • Draw cash in Kathmandu or Nepalgunj. Jumla has banks and the occasional working cash machine, but do not rely on them.
  • Bring snacks, tea, coffee and some staples from Nepalgunj or Jumla; village shops sell little beyond noodles and biscuits.
  • A tent gives you the freedom to stop where the day ends. If you rely on lodges and homestays, let your guide send word ahead.
  • Try Jumla's red marsi rice and, from September, the local apples.
  • Keep your park permit handy; park and army posts at the lake and on the trail ask to see it.
  • Dress modestly in the villages. This is a conservative area and trekkers are still a novelty.

Quick answers

How long does the Rara Lake trek take?

Most people take 8–13 days. The itinerary on this page is 10 days.

How high does the Rara Lake trek go?

The highest point is Murma Top at 3,700 m (12,139 ft). The highest place you sleep is Chere Chaur at 3,055 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. Nothing above about 3,700 m, but long days, three forested passes and very basic food and lodging.

Which permits do I need?

Rara National Park, TIMS card. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, though checks on the trail are inconsistent. Here a guide is practical as well as official: trails braid between villages without signs, very few people speak English, and food and beds have to be arranged house by house.

When is the best time to go?

Apr–May, Oct are best. April and May bring rhododendron and warm days; October has the clearest skies and autumn colour round the lake. November is fine but cold, and March can still have snow on the passes. From December to February snow closes the passes for days at a time and lodges at the lake may shut. July and August are wet, trails are muddy and flights are at their least dependable.
