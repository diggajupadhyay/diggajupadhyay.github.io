Everest Base Camp

The classic walk from Lukla through Namche, Tengboche and Dingboche to Everest Base Camp and the Kala Patthar viewpoint, with two built-in acclimatization days.

Overview

This is the trail most people picture when they think of trekking in Nepal. It follows the Dudh Koshi north from Lukla, climbs to the Sherpa market town of Namche Bazaar and then works its way up the Imja and Khumbu valleys to the lodges at Gorak Shep, a short walk from base camp.

The route is not technical and the paths are wide and well kept. What makes it hard is altitude. You sleep above 4,000 m for five nights and above 5,000 m for one, and the usual itinerary gains height about as fast as is sensible. The two rest days, at Namche and Dingboche, are part of the route rather than optional extras.

Lodges, bakeries and Wi-Fi reach all the way to Gorak Shep, so you carry little and eat well. In return, expect company: in April, May, October and November the trail and the lodges are busy, and beds above Dingboche are worth booking ahead through a guide or agency.

Highlights

  • Namche Bazaar and its Saturday market
  • Tengboche monastery with Ama Dablam behind it
  • Standing on the Khumbu Glacier at base camp
  • Sunset or sunrise over Everest and Nuptse from Kala Patthar
  • Suspension bridges high above the Dudh Koshi

Challenging: Easy underfoot, but nine consecutive nights above 3,400 m make altitude the deciding factor.

Ways to change it

Via Gokyo and the Cho La

Add four to five days to cross from the Gokyo lakes over the 5,420 m Cho La and join the main trail near Lobuche. It makes a loop rather than an out-and-back.

Walk in from the road

Starting from Salleri or Thamdanda adds two to four days through the Solu foothills, skips the flight and gives a gentler start.

Extra night at Lobuche or Dingboche

If you have been to altitude before and know you adapt slowly, a third acclimatization night makes the last push safer.

Permits and guide rules

Guide strongly recommended. Khumbu has not enforced the national guide requirement, so independent trekking remains possible here. A licensed guide still earns their fee on this route: they book beds above Dingboche, watch for altitude symptoms and arrange help quickly if you need to descend.

Sagarmatha National Park

National park

Sagarmatha National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: At the park gate in Monjo, or in advance at the park counter in the Tourist Service Centre, Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

Children under 10 enter free. Nepali press reports from 2024 to 2026 give the same NPR 3,000, 1,500 and 100. Whether 13% VAT is added for foreigners is disputed: an independent guide says tax has been folded into park fees since 2019, while several agency sites still quote NPR 3,390. We found no official statement, so carry the difference in cash. Keep the permit handy; it is checked at the gate.

Khumbu municipality permit

Local permit

Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality entry permit · Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person
SAARC and Chinese nationalsNPR 2,000person

Where: At the municipality's tourist information centre in Lukla when you land, or at the Monjo checkpoint if you walk in from the road. It cannot be bought in Kathmandu.

The municipality calls it a Trek Card: staff take your passport details and a photo, which takes about 15 minutes. It has stood in for the TIMS card in Khumbu since October 2017, when the fee was NPR 2,000. The municipality's own page now lists NPR 3,000, with NPR 2,000 for SAARC and Chinese passport holders, and its 2024–25 visitor and revenue figures fit that split. A few agency pages say every foreigner pays NPR 3,000. Bring rupees in cash.

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

Fly to Lukla from Kathmandu or, in the peak months, from Manthali in Ramechhap. Flights go early in the morning and stop when cloud or wind builds, so delays of a day are common.

  • Flight: Kathmandu to Lukla · 35 min

    Mostly outside the peak months; in spring and autumn many flights move to Manthali.

  • Flight: Manthali to Lukla · 20 min

    Add a four to five hour night drive from Kathmandu to reach the airstrip.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Lukla · 1–2 days + 2 days' walk

    Jeeps reach Salleri and, when the road is passable, Thamdanda below Lukla. A workable fallback when flights are grounded.

Hazards on this route

Altitude illness

Most evacuations on this trail are for altitude sickness, typically between Dingboche and Gorak Shep. Do not climb with symptoms, and descend if they get worse. The aid post at Pheriche can assess you in season.

Lukla flights

Flights are cancelled whenever cloud closes the approach, sometimes for several days. Build in at least one spare day before any international flight, and two in the monsoon shoulder months.

Yak and mule trains

Step to the uphill side of the trail and wait when animals pass, especially on bridges and narrow cliff sections.

Cold above Lobuche

Night temperatures at Gorak Shep fall to around minus 15 °C in spring and autumn, and lower in winter. Lodge bedrooms are unheated.

Practical tips

  • Carry enough cash from Kathmandu or Namche; there are no working ATMs above Namche.
  • Walk slower than feels necessary on the way up. Arriving fresh matters more than arriving first.
  • Drink three to four litres a day and treat all water with tablets, a filter or UV.
  • Hot showers, charging and Wi-Fi cost extra at every lodge and get pricier with height.
  • Use the Dingboche rest day to check how you feel with the altitude self-check before committing to Lobuche.

Quick answers

How long does the Everest Base Camp trek take?

Most people take 12–15 days. The itinerary on this page is 12 days, including 2 days for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Everest Base Camp trek go?

The highest point is Kala Patthar at 5,545 m (18,192 ft). The highest place you sleep is Gorak Shep at 5,164 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it challenging. Easy underfoot, but nine consecutive nights above 3,400 m make altitude the deciding factor.

Which permits do I need?

Sagarmatha National Park, Khumbu municipality permit. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

Khumbu has not enforced the national guide requirement, so independent trekking remains possible here. A licensed guide still earns their fee on this route: they book beds above Dingboche, watch for altitude symptoms and arrange help quickly if you need to descend.

When is the best time to go?

Mar–May, Oct–Nov are best. Autumn has the clearest air; spring is warmer and base camp is busy with expeditions. Winter is dry and very cold above Dingboche. In the monsoon, cloud hides the peaks and Lukla flights are unreliable.
