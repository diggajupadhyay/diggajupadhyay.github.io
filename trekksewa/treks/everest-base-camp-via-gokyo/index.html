Everest Base Camp via Gokyo & Cho La

A 16-day loop from Lukla up the Gokyo valley, over the 5,420 m Cho La to Lobuche, on to Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar, and back down the main trail.

Overview

This route joins the two best-known treks in Khumbu into one loop. You walk up the Gokyo valley to the lakes and climb Gokyo Ri, cross the Ngozumpa Glacier and the Cho La into the Khumbu valley, and then follow the main trail to Gorak Shep, Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar. The way home is the standard descent through Pheriche and Tengboche, so only the two days below Namche are walked twice.

We describe it with Gokyo first. The nights at Gokyo and the climb of Gokyo Ri prepare you for the pass and for sleeping at Lobuche and Gorak Shep, and you go up the steep, loose west side of the Cho La instead of down it. The opposite direction also works, and it is how the pass is crossed on the anticlockwise Three Passes circuit.

The Cho La is what sets this apart from the ordinary base camp trek. It is a seven-hour day with a scramble on unstable rock, a short glacier with crevasses along its edges and no shelter between Thagnak and Dzongla. In good conditions fit trekkers manage it with nothing more technical than microspikes and poles. After fresh snow it should be left alone, so you need either a spare day or the willingness to walk round by Phortse and Pangboche.

Highlights

  • The Gokyo lakes and the view from Gokyo Ri
  • Crossing the Ngozumpa Glacier, the longest in Nepal
  • The Cho La, a 5,420 m pass with a short glacier on its east side
  • Everest Base Camp on the Khumbu Glacier
  • Dawn on Everest and Nuptse from Kala Patthar
  • Tengboche monastery on the way down

Strenuous: Base camp altitude plus a glacier pass at 5,420 m, with ten nights in a row above 4,000 m.

Ways to change it

Base camp first

Walk the main trail to base camp, then cross from Dzongla to Thagnak and descend the Gokyo valley. Acclimatization follows the gentler main-trail schedule, but you come down the loose west side of the pass and finish at the lakes.

Round by Phortse when the pass is closed

From Gokyo or Thagnak go down the east bank to Phortse, traverse to Pangboche and rejoin the base camp trail. It takes two days to reach Dingboche or Pheriche and keeps the rest of the trek intact.

Fifth lake day

Add a day at Gokyo to walk north beside the glacier to the fifth lake, five to seven hours return, for a closer view of Cho Oyu and a wider one of Everest.

The pass decides the season. Late October and November give the firmest, clearest conditions; April and May are warmer with softer snow and afternoon cloud. March can leave the Cho La deep in winter snow, and from mid-December the lodges at Thagnak and Dzongla may shut. In the monsoon the valleys are in cloud and flights are unreliable.

Permits and guide rules

Guide strongly recommended. Khumbu has not enforced the national guide requirement, so the route can be walked independently. The Ngozumpa Glacier crossing and the Cho La are the reason to hire a licensed guide here: neither has a fixed trail, the safe line changes each season, and a guide will also judge whether the pass should be attempted at all on the day.

Sagarmatha National Park

National park

Sagarmatha National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: At the park gate in Monjo, or in advance at the park counter in the Tourist Service Centre, Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

Children under 10 enter free. Nepali press reports from 2024 to 2026 give the same NPR 3,000, 1,500 and 100. Whether 13% VAT is added for foreigners is disputed: an independent guide says tax has been folded into park fees since 2019, while several agency sites still quote NPR 3,390. We found no official statement, so carry the difference in cash. Keep the permit handy; it is checked at the gate.

Khumbu municipality permit

Local permit

Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality entry permit · Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person
SAARC and Chinese nationalsNPR 2,000person

Where: At the municipality's tourist information centre in Lukla when you land, or at the Monjo checkpoint if you walk in from the road. It cannot be bought in Kathmandu.

The municipality calls it a Trek Card: staff take your passport details and a photo, which takes about 15 minutes. It has stood in for the TIMS card in Khumbu since October 2017, when the fee was NPR 2,000. The municipality's own page now lists NPR 3,000, with NPR 2,000 for SAARC and Chinese passport holders, and its 2024–25 visitor and revenue figures fit that split. A few agency pages say every foreigner pays NPR 3,000. Bring rupees in cash.

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

You fly to Lukla to start and from Lukla to finish. Departures are in the first hours of the morning, from Kathmandu or, in peak season, from Manthali in Ramechhap, and they depend entirely on the weather at the Lukla end. Allow for a day's delay in either direction.

  • Flight: Kathmandu to Lukla · 35 min

    The usual route in the quieter months; most peak-season flights are moved to Manthali.

  • Flight: Manthali to Lukla · 20 min

    Leave Kathmandu by road around 1 am, or spend the previous night in Manthali, to make a dawn flight.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Lukla · 1–2 days + 2 days' walk

    Road to Salleri and, conditions permitting, Thamdanda, then the old trail through Paiya and Surke. Slower, but not at the mercy of cloud at Lukla.

Hazards on this route

Altitude illness

You sleep above 4,000 m for ten nights running, and the pass and the two highest nights all come in the second week. The Gokyo side also gains height faster than the main trail. Never cross the Cho La with symptoms; go down the valley you came up.

The Cho La in snow or cloud

Fresh snow hides the path on the glacier and makes the loose west side dangerous, and stones fall on both sides once the sun is on the slopes. Cross early, in clear weather, behind a trodden track. After a storm wait a day or go round by Phortse.

Ngozumpa Glacier crossing

The route over the glacier is unstable rubble on ice beside steep-sided melt ponds. It is re-marked each season as the ice moves. Do not follow old GPS tracks or short-cut across the pond edges.

Four cold nights in a row

The lodges at Thagnak, Dzongla, Lobuche and Gorak Shep are basic, with unheated bedrooms, and nights there are around minus 10 to minus 15 °C in the trekking seasons. Bring a sleeping bag rated to minus 15 °C or lower.

Lukla flights

Cloud at Lukla cancels flights without warning, sometimes for days. Leave at least one spare day, better two, before your international departure.

Practical tips

  • Carry microspikes and poles for the pass, and keep one spare day to sit out bad weather at Thagnak if the Cho La is not in condition.
  • At Gokyo and Thagnak, ask who crossed the pass that day and how long they took. Lodge owners know more than any forecast.
  • Dzongla has only a few lodges. In peak season have your guide phone ahead from Thagnak, or leave early enough to arrive by early afternoon.
  • If you hire a porter, check that they have boots, sunglasses and a warm jacket for the pass. As their employer, that is your job.
  • Bring cash for the whole route from Kathmandu or Namche; nothing higher up dispenses money.
  • Heights quoted for Dole, Machhermo and the Cho La, and the time the pass takes, vary between maps and operators. Treat the figures here as approximate.

Cash machines on the route: Lukla, Namche Bazaar. They run out of cash and go offline, so carry what you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Everest Base Camp via Gokyo & Cho La trek take?

Most people take 15–19 days. The itinerary on this page is 16 days, including 2 days for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Everest Base Camp via Gokyo & Cho La trek go?

The highest point is Kala Patthar at 5,545 m (18,192 ft). The highest place you sleep is Gorak Shep at 5,164 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it strenuous. Base camp altitude plus a glacier pass at 5,420 m, with ten nights in a row above 4,000 m.

Which permits do I need?

Sagarmatha National Park, Khumbu municipality permit. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

Khumbu has not enforced the national guide requirement, so the route can be walked independently. The Ngozumpa Glacier crossing and the Cho La are the reason to hire a licensed guide here: neither has a fixed trail, the safe line changes each season, and a guide will also judge whether the pass should be attempted at all on the day.

When is the best time to go?

Apr–May, Oct–Nov are best. The pass decides the season. Late October and November give the firmest, clearest conditions; April and May are warmer with softer snow and afternoon cloud. March can leave the Cho La deep in winter snow, and from mid-December the lodges at Thagnak and Dzongla may shut. In the monsoon the valleys are in cloud and flights are unreliable.
