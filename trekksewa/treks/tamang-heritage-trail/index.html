Tamang Heritage Trail

A week's loop from Syabrubesi through the Tamang villages of Gatlang, Tatopani, Thuman and Briddim, with homestay nights and a 3,165 m viewpoint at Nagthali.

Langtang: road to Syabrubesi cut by the August 2026 flood

The Bhote Koshi flood of 26 August 2026 badly damaged Syabrubesi and the road to it. Reports in mid-November 2026 said vehicles were stopping at Dhunche or Thulo Syabru, with the Langtang valley and Gosaikunda trails open again from there. The Tamang Heritage Trail runs through the worst-hit area and its status was unclear.

What to do: Confirm the current roadhead and which bridges are in use with your agency or a lodge owner before you book transport.

Overview

The Tamang Heritage Trail is a loop through the villages west and north of Syabrubesi, between the Langtang valley and the Tibetan border. It was laid out in the mid-2000s as a community tourism project, to bring trekkers' money to Tamang and Tibetan-descended villages that the Langtang trail passes by. The point is the villages themselves: stone houses with carved windows, monasteries, mani walls, and evenings in a family kitchen rather than a lodge dining room.

It is a low trek by Nepali standards. You sleep between 1,460 m and 2,600 m and cross one 3,165 m ridge, so altitude is not a concern. The effort is in the gradients: most days include a descent of 500 m or more to a river and a climb of the same size up the other side, on stone steps or dusty track. Rooms are simple, in community lodges and homestays, and the food is what the household eats.

Be clear about its present state. The 2015 earthquake damaged every village on the loop and stopped the hot springs at Tatopani. The Bhote Koshi flood of 26 August 2026 then struck the valley the trail circles: Syabrubesi was badly damaged, Timure and the Rasuwagadhi border post were reported largely destroyed, and bridges and road along the river were lost. The trail was reported open again that autumn, but treat the stages below as the classic plan and confirm each river crossing before you go.

Highlights

  • Homestay nights in Briddim, eating with the family
  • Gatlang, a large and well-kept traditional Tamang village
  • The Nagthali ridge, with views into Tibet and across to Langtang and Ganesh Himal
  • Monasteries, mani walls and chortens between the villages
  • Far fewer trekkers than the Langtang valley next door

Moderate: No altitude to speak of, but nearly every day has a steep descent and a long climb, and the lodging is basic.

Ways to change it

Sleep at Nagthali

Split day four by stopping at the lodges on the Nagthali ridge for sunset and sunrise, then walk down to Thuman next morning. It adds a day and a cold night at 3,165 m.

Combine with the Langtang valley

From Briddim a high trail runs through Khangjim and Sherpagaon to join the Langtang trail at Rimche. Adding Kyanjin Gompa this way makes a trek of 12 to 14 days.

Ride to Gatlang

A dirt road links Syabrubesi with Goljung and Gatlang. When it is open, a jeep saves the first walking day and shortens the loop to six days.

Timure and Rasuwagadhi

Before 2026 some itineraries dropped from Thuman to Timure for a night and a walk to the border fort at Rasuwagadhi. Reports after the flood describe Timure as largely destroyed and the border as closed. Do not plan on this section without current local confirmation.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. Thuman and Timure are on the Department of Immigration's restricted-area list, so this loop is treated here as a restricted-area trek: the permit is issued only through a registered trekking agency and you must walk with a licensed guide. Since 22 March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker. Checks on the ground have been rare and some agencies run the loop on the park permit and TIMS alone, so ask yours what it arranges. After the 2026 flood a guide in touch with the villages is also the only reliable source on which bridges and homestays are open.

Langtang National Park

National park

Langtang National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: At the park checkpoint in Dhunche on the drive to Syabrubesi, or in advance at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

The Nepal Tourism Board and current guides agree on NPR 3,000 and NPR 1,500. Some agencies add 13% VAT to the foreigner fee (NPR 3,390); we could not confirm that it is charged, so carry the difference. NPR 100 for Nepali citizens is the standard mountain-park rate; the tourism board's page still shows an older figure.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.

Rasuwa border RAP

Restricted area

Rasuwa (Thuman and Timure) restricted area permit · Department of Immigration

All foreign nationalsUSD 20person per week

Where: Department of Immigration in Kathmandu, through a registered trekking agency only.

Applies to the border area north of Syabrubesi, which the fee table defines as Gosaikunda Rural Municipality ward 1 and part of ward 2. The main Langtang valley, Gosaikunda and Helambu trails do not need it. Since 22 March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker, through a registered agency and with a licensed guide.

Getting there

By road to Syabrubesi, the same seven to nine hour journey from Machhapokhari in Kathmandu as for the Langtang valley. The Bhote Koshi flood of 26 August 2026 cut the road short of the town and took out the bridges over the river. The loop was reported open again later that autumn, once a temporary footbridge had been built at Syabrubesi, but the road was still reported closed in mid-November 2026. Ask your agency how groups are reaching the start.

  • Bus: Kathmandu to Syabrubesi · 7–9 h

    The classic approach, by local or express bus. Reported closed short of Syabrubesi since the August 2026 flood; check whether it has reopened.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Dhunche · 5–7 h

    The roadhead in use since the flood. Syabrubesi is a further 15 km down a damaged road; ask what is passable on wheels and what has to be walked.

Hazards on this route

Flood damage along the Bhote Koshi

The flood of 26 August 2026 destroyed bridges, long stretches of road and riverside buildings between the border and Syabrubesi. The villages on the loop sit high above the river, but the start, the finish and the Thuman to Briddim day all depend on river crossings. Do not set out without current information from someone on the ground.

Steep descents

The drops from Gatlang to Chilime, from Nagthali to Thuman and from Thuman to the river are each 500 to 800 m on stone steps and loose track. They are slippery after rain. Use poles and do not hurry the afternoons.

Restricted border area

The loop runs within a few kilometres of the Tibetan border. Stay on the trekking route, carry your passport and permits, and do not photograph army or police posts.

Slow evacuation

There is no hospital on the loop; the nearest is in Dhunche. With the valley road damaged, an evacuation is likely to mean a helicopter, so carry a proper first-aid kit and insurance that covers air rescue.

Practical tips

  • In a homestay you eat what the family cooks, at the family's hours. Agree the price for bed and meals when you arrive.
  • Carry all the cash you need, in small notes. There is no ATM on the loop and nobody can change large bills.
  • Ask in Gatlang whether the Tatopani pools have water before you count on a soak.
  • Dress modestly in the villages, and ask before photographing people, houses or the inside of a monastery.
  • Bring a light sleeping bag or liner. Homestay bedding is clean but thin, and nights at Tatopani and Nagthali are cold.

We know of no cash machines on this route. Carry all the rupees you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Tamang Heritage Trail trek take?

Most people take 6–9 days. The itinerary on this page is 7 days.

How high does the Tamang Heritage Trail trek go?

The highest point is Nagthali at 3,165 m (10,384 ft). The highest place you sleep is Tatopani at 2,607 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. No altitude to speak of, but nearly every day has a steep descent and a long climb, and the lodging is basic.

Which permits do I need?

Langtang National Park, TIMS card, Rasuwa border RAP. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

Thuman and Timure are on the Department of Immigration's restricted-area list, so this loop is treated here as a restricted-area trek: the permit is issued only through a registered trekking agency and you must walk with a licensed guide. Since 22 March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker. Checks on the ground have been rare and some agencies run the loop on the park permit and TIMS alone, so ask yours what it arranges. After the 2026 flood a guide in touch with the villages is also the only reliable source on which bridges and homestays are open.

When is the best time to go?

Mar–Apr, Oct–Nov are best. October and November are the clearest months, and March and April bring rhododendron on the Nagthali ridge. Winter is cold but walkable, with occasional snow on the ridge. May is hot on the lower climbs. In the monsoon the trails are slippery, leeches are out, and the valley road is at its most fragile.
