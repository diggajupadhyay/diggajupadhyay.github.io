Mohare Danda

Five or six days through Magar farming villages west of Ghorepani, staying in community lodges and homestays, to a hilltop at 3,300 m with a view from Dhaulagiri to Machhapuchhre.

Overview

The Mohare Danda trail was laid out by villagers in Myagdi and Parbat districts, led by the Nangi teacher Mahabir Pun, to bring trekking income to places the main routes had bypassed. It climbs from the Kali Gandaki through terraced Magar villages to a forested summit at about 3,300 m, a little higher than Poon Hill and only a few kilometres from it in a straight line. The view is much the same, from Dhaulagiri round to Machhapuchhre, and on most mornings you share it with a handful of people.

The places you stay are the point of the trek as much as the scenery. Each night is spent in a community lodge or a family homestay managed by a village committee, with fixed prices, and the surplus pays for schools, health posts and the trail itself. Rooms are simple and often shared. Meals are whatever the kitchen is cooking, usually dal bhat with vegetables from the garden, and evenings are spent with the families who run the place. Do not expect menus, bakeries or hot showers on demand.

The walking is steady rather than hard: about 2,400 m of ascent spread over three days, on village paths and forest trails with few signs. Stages are flexible because the lodges are close together, and the route can end at the road in Tiplyang or continue along the ridge to Ghorepani and Poon Hill. Details change from year to year as roads advance and lodges open or close, so confirm the current stops with a guide or the lodge network before setting out.

Highlights

  • Sunrise from Mohare Danda, from Dhaulagiri round to Machhapuchhre
  • Nights in village-run lodges and Magar homestays
  • Nangi village and the school at its centre
  • Rhododendron forest on the ridge in spring
  • Trails with very few other trekkers

Ways to change it

On to Ghorepani and Poon Hill

From Mohare Danda a forested ridge trail leads north-east to Ghorepani in five to six hours. Visit Poon Hill the next morning and walk out by Ulleri to Tikhedhunga, or carry on to Tadapani and Ghandruk.

Shorter approach by jeep

Jeep tracks have reached several of the lower villages. Some groups ride as far as Nangi and do the trek in three or four days, missing the walk up from the river.

Link to Khopra Danda

The community trail continues from Ghorepani to Swanta and Khopra Danda. Allow nine to ten days for the whole route from Galeshwor.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. The upper part of the trail lies in the Annapurna Conservation Area, where the Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to walk with a licensed guide since April 2023; checks on the trail are inconsistent. A guide is worth having here in any case: the paths are barely signposted, and someone needs to phone each village so the lodge knows you are coming.

ACAP

Conservation area

Annapurna Conservation Area Permit · National Trust for Nature Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, single entry

Where: Online at NTNC's e-permit site, or in person at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu, and the Nepal Tourism Board office in Pokhara. A permit issued at a trail checkpost costs double.

The fee includes tax and children under 10 do not need a permit. Paying online by card adds a 2.9% gateway charge. Covers every trek in the Annapurna range and Lower Mustang; Upper Mustang and Nar Phu need a restricted-area permit as well.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.

Agency required

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

Buses and jeeps run from Pokhara to Beni in about three hours, and Galeshwor is fifteen minutes further up the Jomsom road. The trail ends on the same road at Tiplyang, where you can flag down a bus or have a jeep meet you for the drive back.

  • Bus: Pokhara to Beni · 3–4 hr

    Frequent buses from the Baglung bus park. Change in Beni for a local jeep or taxi to Galeshwor.

  • Jeep: Pokhara to Galeshwor · 3–3.5 hr

    Private jeep to the bridge where the trail starts.

  • Bus: Tiplyang to Pokhara · 4–5 hr

    Buses from Jomsom and Tatopani pass through. Seats are easier to find in the morning.

Hazards on this route

Poorly marked trails

Signposting is sparse and village paths branch constantly. Without a guide or a GPS track it is easy to lose an hour, and in the forest above Nangi a wrong turn can take you well off route.

Rain, leeches and slippery paths

In the monsoon, and after any heavy rain, the clay paths and stone steps are slick, and leeches are active below about 2,500 m.

Cold on the summit

Mohare Danda is exposed and the lodge is basic. Nights fall below freezing from November to March, and snow can lie on the top in mid-winter.

Few services

There are no cash machines, pharmacies or gear shops after Beni, which also has the nearest hospital. Carry your own first-aid kit and a way to treat water.

Practical tips

  • Book through the community lodge network or an agency so that each village knows you are coming; kitchens buy food for the number of guests expected.
  • Carry cash in small notes. Prices are fixed and posted, and nobody can change large notes.
  • Pack a sleeping bag liner and a warm jacket. Blankets are provided, but the rooms are unheated.
  • Ask before photographing people, and dress modestly in the villages.
  • Bring a refillable bottle and water treatment; most lodges do not sell bottled water.
  • Carry your ACAP permit and TIMS card. The lower trail is outside the conservation area but the ridge and any onward route to Ghorepani are inside it, and advice on what is checked varies.

We know of no cash machines on this route. Carry all the rupees you need from Pokhara. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Mohare Danda trek take?

Most people take 5–8 days. The itinerary on this page is 6 days.

How high does the Mohare Danda trek go?

The highest point is Mohare Danda at 3,300 m (10,827 ft). The highest place you sleep is Mohare Danda at 3,300 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. Low altitude and modest daily distances, but three consecutive uphill days on rough village and forest paths.

Which permits do I need?

ACAP, TIMS card. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

The upper part of the trail lies in the Annapurna Conservation Area, where the Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to walk with a licensed guide since April 2023; checks on the trail are inconsistent. A guide is worth having here in any case: the paths are barely signposted, and someone needs to phone each village so the lodge knows you are coming.

When is the best time to go?

Mar–Apr, Oct–Nov are best. October to December has the clearest skies, and March and April bring rhododendron bloom on the ridge. The low altitude keeps the trail open in winter, though snow sometimes lies on the summit in January and February. The monsoon months are wet, with leeches in the forest and cloud on the top.
