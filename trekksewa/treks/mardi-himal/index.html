Mardi Himal

A short ridge trek from Kande through forest to High Camp, with a dawn climb to the 4,200 m viewpoint or the 4,500 m base camp below Mardi Himal and Machhapuchhre.

Overview

Mardi Himal is the pointed 5,587 m peak at the end of Machhapuchhre's south-west ridge. The trek climbs the forested spur below it, between the Modi Khola and the Mardi Khola, and comes out above the trees on a narrow grass ridge that leads almost to the foot of the two mountains. It opened as a lodge route in 2012 and has become the favourite short trek for people who want to get high and close in under a week.

The first two days are almost entirely in forest, with no villages after Pitam Deurali and only clusters of lodges named for what they are: Forest Camp, Low Camp, High Camp. Above Low Camp the trees give way and the ridge narrows, with Annapurna South and Hiunchuli across the valley to the left and Machhapuchhre straight ahead. The final morning follows the crest from High Camp to the Upper Viewpoint, where most people turn round, or on for another hour or more to the marker called base camp.

It is a short trek but not a soft one. The plan below gains over 1,400 m of sleeping height in two days and reaches 4,500 m on the fourth morning, which is fast. The upper ridge is exposed to wind, sudden cloud and, from December to March, snow. Lodges are simple and few at each stop, and water is scarce at High Camp. The steep descent to Siding and a jeep down the Mardi Khola turn it into a loop.

Highlights

  • Machhapuchhre from a few kilometres away on the upper ridge
  • Sunrise on Annapurna South and Hiunchuli from High Camp
  • Two days of quiet oak and rhododendron forest
  • Open ridge walking between Low Camp and High Camp
  • A loop that starts an hour's drive from Pokhara

Moderate: Short and well marked, but it gains height fast, reaches 4,500 m and finishes along a narrow, exposed ridge.

Ways to change it

Four days

Fit, acclimatized walkers go from Kande to Forest Camp on day one and to High Camp on day two, then descend to Siding and Pokhara on day four. It leaves no margin for altitude or weather.

Start from Dhampus

A slightly longer first day from Dhampus through Pothana, on a village trail with wide views.

Descend to Landruk

From Forest Camp a steep trail drops west to Landruk, which links to Jhinu Danda and the Annapurna Base Camp route or across the valley to Ghandruk.

Extra night on the ridge

Splitting the climb with a night at Badal Danda, or spending a second night at High Camp, makes the summit morning easier and gives a spare day for weather.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. Since April 2023 the Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers in conservation areas to walk with a licensed guide, though checks on the trail are inconsistent. On this route a guide matters most on the ridge above High Camp, where cloud can close in within minutes and the path is hard to follow under snow.

ACAP

Conservation area

Annapurna Conservation Area Permit · National Trust for Nature Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, single entry

Where: Online at NTNC's e-permit site, or in person at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu, and the Nepal Tourism Board office in Pokhara. A permit issued at a trail checkpost costs double.

The fee includes tax and children under 10 do not need a permit. Paying online by card adds a 2.9% gateway charge. Covers every trek in the Annapurna range and Lower Mustang; Upper Mustang and Nar Phu need a restricted-area permit as well.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.

Agency required

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

Kande is an hour by road from Pokhara on the Baglung highway. The trek ends at Siding in the Mardi Khola valley, where jeeps take about two hours to return to Pokhara on a rough track.

  • Drive: Pokhara to Kande · 1 hr

    Taxi, or any Baglung-bound bus from the Baglung bus park.

  • Jeep: Pokhara to Dhampus · 1–1.5 hr

    An alternative start that joins the main trail at Pothana after about an hour and a half on foot.

  • Jeep: Siding to Pokhara · 2–2.5 hr

    Shared jeeps leave when full, usually around midday. Ask your lodge at Low Camp to phone ahead.

Hazards on this route

Fast ascent

From Low Camp to base camp is a gain of 1,500 m in under two days. Headache and nausea at High Camp are common. Do not go above High Camp with symptoms, and if they worsen in the night, go down to Low Camp.

Exposed ridge and sudden cloud

Above High Camp the path follows a narrow crest with steep grass and rock on both sides. Cloud often arrives by nine or ten and visibility can fall to a few metres. Under snow or ice the ridge is dangerous without proper footwear, so turn back rather than push on.

Winter snow

From late December into March the ridge above Badal Danda is often snowbound and the High Camp lodges may close. Ask in Pokhara or at Low Camp before committing.

Little water, few beds

High Camp has little water and not many rooms, and in peak season people sleep in the dining halls. Carry enough water for the summit morning, because there is none on the ridge.

Practical tips

  • Carry at least two litres of water on the forest days and the summit morning; sources between the camps are unreliable.
  • Take microspikes and warm gloves from November to March.
  • The Upper Viewpoint gives nearly the same view as base camp. Go on only if the sky is clear and you feel strong.
  • Lodges are basic above Forest Camp. Bring a sleeping bag and a power bank.
  • Carry cash from Pokhara; there is nowhere to get money on the ridge.
  • Heights quoted for the camps differ by up to 100 m between maps and lodge signboards, so treat them as approximate.

Quick answers

How long does the Mardi Himal trek take?

Most people take 4–7 days. The itinerary on this page is 5 days.

How high does the Mardi Himal trek go?

The highest point is Mardi Himal Base Camp at 4,500 m (14,764 ft). The highest place you sleep is Mardi Himal High Camp at 3,550 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. Short and well marked, but it gains height fast, reaches 4,500 m and finishes along a narrow, exposed ridge.

Which permits do I need?

ACAP, TIMS card. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

Since April 2023 the Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers in conservation areas to walk with a licensed guide, though checks on the trail are inconsistent. On this route a guide matters most on the ridge above High Camp, where cloud can close in within minutes and the path is hard to follow under snow.

When is the best time to go?

Mar–Apr, Oct–Nov are best. October and November are the clearest months, and April brings rhododendron bloom in the forest. Cloud usually covers the ridge by late morning in any season, which is why the summit day starts in the dark. From late December to February snow often blocks the ridge above High Camp. The monsoon forest is wet, full of leeches and without views.
