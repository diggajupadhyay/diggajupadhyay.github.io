Everest Three Passes

An 18-day loop from Lukla across three passes above 5,300 m, taking in Chhukung, Everest Base Camp, Kala Patthar, the Gokyo lakes and the Thame valley.

Overview

The Three Passes route is the complete circuit of Khumbu. It links the three high valleys above Namche, the Imja, the Khumbu and the Gokyo, by crossing the Kongma La (5,535 m), the Cho La (5,420 m) and the Renjo La (5,360 m), and it collects Everest Base Camp, Kala Patthar and Gokyo Ri on the way. Very little ground is repeated: you go up the busy main trail and come home down the quiet valley from Thame.

This guide describes it anticlockwise, Kongma La first. That way the first week follows the well-tested base camp acclimatization schedule, with the Pheriche aid post and plenty of lodges close by if altitude does not suit you, and you climb the Cho La and the Renjo La from their high sides: about 600 m of ascent on each instead of 720 m and nearly 1,000 m. The price is that the highest and roughest pass comes first, and that you descend, rather than climb, the loose slopes on the west of the Kongma La and the Cho La.

None of the passes needs a rope in normal conditions, but each is a long day that goes above 5,300 m with no lodge between start and finish, and the Cho La crosses a glacier. Snow can close any of them for a day or two. Come with spare days, microspikes and previous experience of trekking above 4,500 m, and be ready to walk round a pass by the valley trail if conditions say so. Walking times on the pass days, and even the heights printed for the passes, differ between maps and operators, so treat the figures here as a guide.

Highlights

  • Three crossings above 5,300 m, each into a different valley
  • Chhukung Ri and the south wall of Lhotse
  • Everest Base Camp and the view from Kala Patthar
  • The Gokyo lakes and the Ngozumpa Glacier
  • Everest, Lhotse and Makalu lined up from the top of the Renjo La
  • The quiet walk out through Thame

Strenuous: Three pass days of six to nine hours, each crossing above 5,300 m, a glacier on the Cho La and ten nights above 4,400 m.

Ways to change it

Clockwise, Renjo La first

Walk up the Thame valley, cross the Renjo La to Gokyo, then the Cho La and Kongma La. The passes come in order of difficulty and Everest appears suddenly at the top of the first. The cost is a 1,000 m climb from Lungden and a first week with fewer lodges and less help close by.

Walking round a pass

Each pass has a valley alternative. Chhukung to Lobuche goes through Dingboche and Thukla in about six hours. Dzongla to Gokyo goes down to Pangboche or Phortse and up the Gokyo valley in three days. Gokyo to Namche goes down through Dole in two.

A slower first week

Add a second night at Dingboche with a walk up Nangkartshang before moving to Chhukung. It is worth the day if you have not been above 4,500 m before.

Fifth lake day

With a spare day at Gokyo, walk north along the glacier to the fifth lake for the view of Cho Oyu and Everest. Five to seven hours return.

Permits and guide rules

Guide strongly recommended. Khumbu has not enforced the national guide requirement, and experienced trekkers do walk this circuit independently. The passes are where a licensed guide matters most: the Kongma La and the Cho La cross moraine and glacier with no fixed trail, the line shifts every season, and in cloud or fresh snow people lose it within minutes. Do not cross any of the three alone.

Sagarmatha National Park

National park

Sagarmatha National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: At the park gate in Monjo, or in advance at the park counter in the Tourist Service Centre, Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

Children under 10 enter free. Nepali press reports from 2024 to 2026 give the same NPR 3,000, 1,500 and 100. Whether 13% VAT is added for foreigners is disputed: an independent guide says tax has been folded into park fees since 2019, while several agency sites still quote NPR 3,390. We found no official statement, so carry the difference in cash. Keep the permit handy; it is checked at the gate.

Khumbu municipality permit

Local permit

Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality entry permit · Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person
SAARC and Chinese nationalsNPR 2,000person

Where: At the municipality's tourist information centre in Lukla when you land, or at the Monjo checkpoint if you walk in from the road. It cannot be bought in Kathmandu.

The municipality calls it a Trek Card: staff take your passport details and a photo, which takes about 15 minutes. It has stood in for the TIMS card in Khumbu since October 2017, when the fee was NPR 2,000. The municipality's own page now lists NPR 3,000, with NPR 2,000 for SAARC and Chinese passport holders, and its 2024–25 visitor and revenue figures fit that split. A few agency pages say every foreigner pays NPR 3,000. Bring rupees in cash.

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

Fly in and out of Lukla. Flights leave early from Kathmandu or, during the spring and autumn peaks, from Manthali in Ramechhap, and they stop as soon as cloud or wind builds. On a trek this long, lose a day to the weather at the start rather than hurry the acclimatization to make it up.

  • Flight: Kathmandu to Lukla · 35 min

    Mostly in the quieter months; in peak season the majority of flights use Manthali.

  • Flight: Manthali to Lukla · 20 min

    Four to five hours by road from Kathmandu, usually driven in the small hours for a dawn departure.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Lukla · 1–2 days + 2 days' walk

    Jeep to Salleri and, when the track allows, Thamdanda, then walk in through Paiya and Surke. Useful when Lukla is closed, and the extra days low down do no harm.

Hazards on this route

Altitude illness

You spend ten nights above 4,400 m and go above 5,300 m on seven separate days. Problems usually show up between Dingboche and Lobuche, around the first pass. Do not cross a pass with symptoms: the far side may be lower, but it is a long way from help.

Snow, ice and cloud on the passes

Fresh snow hides the trail, loads the steep slopes and turns the rock on the Cho La and Kongma La to ice. After a storm wait a day for other parties and the sun to open the route. If cloud comes down while you are high, stop and wait for a gap; do not guess the line.

Glacier and moraine crossings

The Cho La glacier has crevasses near its edges, and the steep slopes on both sides of the pass shed stones once the sun reaches them. The Khumbu and Ngozumpa crossings are on rubble over ice that shifts every season. Stay on the trodden line and cross early in the day.

Long days with no shelter

Each pass day is six to nine hours with no lodge, no certain water and no easy exit. Start by six, carry two litres, food, a headlamp and a warm layer, and set a time by which you turn back if you are not on top.

Lukla flights

Flights are cancelled whenever cloud closes the approach, sometimes for several days. Keep spare days at the end as well as on the passes, and do not book an international flight for the day after you plan to fly out.

Practical tips

  • Build in two spare days. One usually goes on waiting for a pass to clear and the other on Lukla.
  • Carry microspikes and trekking poles for the Cho La. They weigh little and decide whether an icy morning is safe.
  • The evening before each pass, ask your lodge owner who came over that day and what the conditions were. It is the best forecast you will get.
  • Bring cash for the whole trek from Kathmandu or Namche. Prices rise with height, and the small lodges at Dzongla, Thagnak and Lungden take nothing else.
  • Keep water bottles inside your jacket on pass mornings; hoses and open bottles freeze within the first hour.
  • If you are short of time, drop the Kongma La, not the rest days.

Cash machines on the route: Lukla, Namche Bazaar. They run out of cash and go offline, so carry what you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Everest Three Passes trek take?

Most people take 17–21 days. The itinerary on this page is 18 days, including 2 days for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Everest Three Passes trek go?

The highest point is Kala Patthar at 5,545 m (18,192 ft). The highest place you sleep is Gorak Shep at 5,164 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it strenuous. Three pass days of six to nine hours, each crossing above 5,300 m, a glacier on the Cho La and ten nights above 4,400 m.

Which permits do I need?

Sagarmatha National Park, Khumbu municipality permit. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

Khumbu has not enforced the national guide requirement, and experienced trekkers do walk this circuit independently. The passes are where a licensed guide matters most: the Kongma La and the Cho La cross moraine and glacier with no fixed trail, the line shifts every season, and in cloud or fresh snow people lose it within minutes. Do not cross any of the three alone.

When is the best time to go?

Apr–May, Oct–Nov are best. Late October and November are the most reliable for all three passes. April and May are warmer, with afternoon cloud and softer snow. In March the passes often still hold deep winter snow, and from December the lodges at Dzongla, Thagnak and Lungden may close. A single storm can shut a pass for several days in any month.
