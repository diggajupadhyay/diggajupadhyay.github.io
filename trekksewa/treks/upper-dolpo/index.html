Upper Dolpo

A three-week camping journey from Juphal past Phoksundo Lake, over the 5,350 m Kang La to Shey Gompa and Saldang, returning by the Jeng La and the Tarap valley.

Overview

Upper Dolpo is the high, dry country north of the Dhaulagiri and Kanjiroba ranges, Tibetan in culture and cut off by snow for half the year. This route is the usual way in: up the Suligad gorge to Phoksundo Lake, along the cliff path above its western shore, and over the Kang La, about 5,350 m, to the red-walled monastery of Shey below the Crystal Mountain. It then loops east through Namgung and Saldang, crosses the Jeng La to the wide Tarap valley and follows a long gorge back down to Dunai.

It is a serious undertaking. You cross three passes above 5,000 m, camp for most of three weeks and spend ten days beyond Ringmo with no road, clinic or dependable phone. A full crew with tents, a cook and pack animals is the normal way to travel; the few homestays and tea shops at Shey, Saldang and Dho Tarap cannot be counted on. The restricted-area permit costs USD 500 for the first ten days, which shapes how agencies plan the route.

The stage plan below is the one most crews follow, but camps are chosen by water, grazing and how the animals are going, and published altitudes for several of them disagree by hundreds of metres. We could not cross-check every figure beyond Saldang. Use the numbers for planning, not navigation, and expect your guide to adjust.

Highlights

  • Phoksundo Lake and the cliff trail above its western shore
  • Crossing the Kang La into the Shey valley
  • Shey Gompa and the pilgrim circuit of the Crystal Mountain
  • Saldang and Namgung, farming villages on the edge of the Tibetan plateau
  • The broad Tarap valley with its Bon and Buddhist monasteries
  • Blue sheep on the slopes and, with luck, the tracks of a snow leopard

Strenuous: Three passes over 5,000 m, three weeks of camping and long stretches where the only way out is to keep walking.

Ways to change it

Out and back to Shey

Return from Shey over the Kang La to Ringmo and Juphal in about 16 days. It needs fewer days on the expensive permit but repeats the hardest pass.

Yangze Gompa

From Saldang, a day's walk north along the river leads to the old monastery at Yangze. Crews often add it and return through Saldang to Sibu.

By the Numa La and Baga La

Instead of the Tarap gorge, some groups return from Dho Tarap over the Numa La and Baga La, both above 5,000 m, to Ringmo, closing a loop through the lake country.

East to Jomsom

A longer traverse continues east from Upper Dolpo through Chharka Bhot and over more 5,000 m passes to the Kali Gandaki at Jomsom, taking close to four weeks in all.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. Upper and Lower Dolpo are restricted areas: the permits are issued only through a registered trekking agency and you must walk with a licensed guide. In practice you need a crew as well. Trails beyond the lake are unmarked, villages are a day or more apart, and river levels, snow on the passes and the condition of the pack animals decide each day's plan.

Upper Dolpo RAP

Restricted area

Upper Dolpo restricted area permit · Department of Immigration

All foreign nationalsUSD 500person for the first 10 days, then USD 50 a day

Where: Department of Immigration in Kathmandu, through a registered trekking agency only.

Unlike Upper Mustang, Upper Dolpo is still listed at USD 500 for the first ten days in the Department of Immigration's fee table. Several 2026 agency pages claim it has also moved to USD 50 a day; the official table does not show that, so confirm with your agency before you budget. Since 22 March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker, but you still need a registered agency and a licensed guide (at most seven trekkers per guide). The Shey Phoksundo park permit and the Lower Dolpo permit for the approach are extra.

Agency required

Restricted area

Lower Dolpo restricted area permit · Department of Immigration

All foreign nationalsUSD 20person per week, then USD 5 a day

Since 22 March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker; before that the minimum was two. You still have to book through a registered agency and walk with a licensed guide, with at most seven trekkers per guide. The Shey Phoksundo park permit is extra.

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry

Where: Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu, or the park entry post on the trail in from Juphal.

The Nepal Tourism Board page gives NPR 3,000 and NPR 1,500 per entry; whether VAT is added on top is unconfirmed, as for the other national parks. Park headquarters are at Sumduwa. The Dolpo restricted-area permits are separate.

Getting there

Fly from Kathmandu to Nepalgunj, spend the night, and take the early flight to Juphal in Dolpa next morning. Juphal flights run only in calm, clear weather and are least reliable in the monsoon, which is also the best season for the trek, so allow spare days at both ends. Crews and pack animals are usually arranged to meet you in Juphal or Dunai.

  • Flight: Kathmandu to Nepalgunj · about 1 hour

    Several flights a day. Buses take ten to twelve hours.

  • Flight: Nepalgunj to Juphal · about 40 min

    Small aircraft, early morning only, and frequently delayed or cancelled by wind and cloud.

  • Jeep: Nepalgunj to Dunai · about 2 days

    The Bheri valley road through Jajarkot is rough, mostly a dry-season route and often cut in the monsoon, when this trek is at its best.

Hazards on this route

Altitude with no easy way down

You sleep above 4,000 m for about nine nights and cross 5,350 m on the eighth day. Once over the Kang La, going down means crossing another pass. Carry a full altitude kit that your guide knows how to use, and do not cross any pass with symptoms.

Snow on the Kang La

The pass can hold snow into June and close again from late October. Pack animals cannot cross deep snow, so a blocked pass may mean waiting or turning back. Build spare days into the plan.

River crossings

The stream in the gorge below the Kang La, and side streams elsewhere, are waded. They rise sharply on warm afternoons and after rain. Cross early in the day, unclip your pack and use poles.

Isolation

Between Ringmo and Dho Tarap there is no dependable communication or medical help. A satellite phone or messenger with the group, and insurance that covers helicopter evacuation, are basic requirements.

Cliff trails

The path above Phoksundo Lake and parts of the Tarap gorge are narrow with long drops. Stand on the uphill side when animals pass and take your time on loose sections.

Practical tips

  • Plan the days against the permit: the USD 500 fee covers the first ten days in Upper Dolpo and each further day costs USD 50. Your agency sets the entry and exit dates.
  • Bring a sleeping bag rated to about minus 15 °C and a proper down jacket, even in July. Clear nights at 4,500 m freeze.
  • Sun and wind are fierce on the plateau. Pack a wide hat, good sunglasses, lip balm and more sunscreen than you think you need.
  • Carry river sandals and a small dry bag for the wading days.
  • Walk clockwise round Buddhist chortens and mani walls but anticlockwise round Bon ones; your guide will know which is which.
  • Agree before you leave how many spare days the crew is paid for, because Juphal flights are often delayed on the way out.

Quick answers

How long does the Upper Dolpo trek take?

Most people take 20–27 days. The itinerary on this page is 22 days, including 3 days for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Upper Dolpo trek go?

The highest point is Kang La (Dolpo) at 5,350 m (17,552 ft). The highest place you sleep is Jeng La Phedi at 4,600 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it strenuous. Three passes over 5,000 m, three weeks of camping and long stretches where the only way out is to keep walking.

Which permits do I need?

Upper Dolpo RAP, Lower Dolpo RAP, Shey Phoksundo National Park. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

Upper and Lower Dolpo are restricted areas: the permits are issued only through a registered trekking agency and you must walk with a licensed guide. In practice you need a crew as well. Trails beyond the lake are unmarked, villages are a day or more apart, and river levels, snow on the passes and the condition of the pack animals decide each day's plan.

When is the best time to go?

Jun–Sep are best.
