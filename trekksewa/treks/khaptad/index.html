Khaptad National Park

A short trek from Silgadhi in Doti up to the rolling 3,000 m grasslands of Khaptad National Park, with wide views of Api and Saipal and almost no other foreign visitors.

Overview

Khaptad is a plateau of grassland and forest at about 3,000 m where four far-western districts, Doti, Achham, Bajhang and Bajura, meet. Twenty-two open meadows, called patans, are set among oak, fir, hemlock and rhododendron, and in clear weather the snow peaks of Api and Saipal line the northern horizon. The park takes its name from Khaptad Baba, a hermit who lived and taught here for about fifty years and whose ashram is still a place of pilgrimage.

The usual approach is from Silgadhi, the old hill bazaar above Dipayal. A day's walk, or a short jeep ride, brings you to the park gate at Jhigrana, and a second day's climb through forest reaches the park headquarters on the plateau. Most visitors give a full day to the meadows, the Tribeni temple and the small lake of Khaptad Daha before going back the same way.

Getting here is the hard part: a flight to Dhangadhi and a full day on hill roads. On the plateau, accommodation is a handful of basic lodges and park buildings near the headquarters, with few supplies, so some groups still carry tents and food. In return you get gentle walking, big skies and a trek that Nepalis know far better than foreigners do.

Ways to change it

Drive to the gate

Taking a jeep between Silgadhi and Jhigrana in both directions cuts the trip to four or five days from Dhangadhi.

Out through Bajhang

Instead of returning to Jhigrana, a trail descends north from the plateau towards the road in Bajhang, a day or two away, making a traverse. It is little used by foreigners and needs a local guide.

From Achham or Bajura

Other trails climb to the plateau from Sanfebagar in Achham and from Bajura. They are longer and used mainly by pilgrims.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers in protected areas to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, though checks on the trail are inconsistent and rare this far west. A guide is still worth having here: there are few signs on the plateau, mist comes down quickly over the meadows, and beds and meals need arranging in advance.

Getting there

Fly from Kathmandu to Dhangadhi in the far-western Terai, then drive seven to nine hours into the hills to Silgadhi in Doti. Buses and shared jeeps cover the same road. A jeep track continues from Silgadhi to the park gate at Jhigrana.

  • Flight: Kathmandu to Dhangadhi · about 1 hr 15 min

    Several flights a day. The road from Kathmandu takes fourteen hours or more.

  • Drive: Dhangadhi to Silgadhi · 7–9 hours

    A paved hill road by way of Budar and Dipayal. Landslides can close it in the monsoon.

  • Jeep: Silgadhi to Jhigrana · 1.5–2 hours

    A rough track that saves the first walking day.

Hazards on this route

Mist and navigation

Cloud can cover the plateau within minutes, and the meadows look alike in every direction. Stay on the main paths, keep the group together and do not wander far from headquarters alone.

Winter snow

From December to February snow lies deep on the plateau and on the trail above Bichpani. Lodges may be shut and the route unbroken; go only with local advice.

Road travel

The drive from Dhangadhi is long, on narrow hill roads with heavy bus traffic and monsoon landslides. Travel by day and leave slack in your return plans.

Few supplies, distant care

There is no real shop above Jhigrana, and the nearest hospitals are down in Silgadhi and Dipayal. Carry a first-aid kit, water treatment and spare food.

Practical tips

  • Buy supplies in Dhangadhi or Silgadhi and carry snacks and some staples up with you.
  • Bring a warm sleeping bag. Nights at 3,000 m are cold even in May, and lodge bedding is thin.
  • Alcohol, tobacco and the killing of animals are not allowed in the zone around the ashram. Follow the rule, and ask before you bring meat up.
  • Carry small notes; nobody on the plateau can change large bills.
  • The park fee is disputed between sources, and some agencies also issue a TIMS card for Khaptad. Carry extra cash for the gate and let your agency decide on the card.

Cash machines on the route: Silgadhi. They run out of cash and go offline, so carry what you need from Dhangadhi. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Khaptad National Park trek take?

Most people take 5–9 days. The itinerary on this page is 6 days, including 1 day for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Khaptad National Park trek go?

The highest point is Khaptad at 3,020 m (9,908 ft). The highest place you sleep is Khaptad at 3,020 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. Two steady uphill days on good forest trails to about 3,000 m; the long road journey is more tiring than the walking.

Which permits do I need?
