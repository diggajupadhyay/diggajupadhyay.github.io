Jiri to Everest Base Camp

The old approach to Everest on foot: from Jiri over the Lamjura La and through Junbesi and Kharikhola to join the main trail near Lukla, then on to base camp and Kala Patthar.

Overview

Before the airstrip at Lukla took over, everyone bound for Everest walked in through the middle hills. The 1953 expedition set out on foot from the edge of the Kathmandu Valley; after the Swiss-built road reached Jiri in the 1980s, the walk began there. This route follows that trail east across the grain of the country, through the Sherpa villages of Solu, joins the main trail just north of Lukla and continues to base camp.

The trailhead has kept moving ever since. Buses still stop in Jiri, but the road now runs on to Shivalaya, a rough track continues to Bhandar and Kinja, and jeeps from the Salleri side reach Junbesi, Nunthala and Kharikhola. Most people today start walking at Shivalaya, and some take a jeep as far as Bhandar. The itinerary below begins in Jiri so that the whole classic route is covered: skip day 2 if you ride to Shivalaya, and days 2 and 3 if you ride to Bhandar.

What the extra week buys you is strong legs, a slow change from terraced farms to high country, and villages that see a handful of trekkers a day instead of hundreds. What it costs is effort. The trail crosses three ridges and the deep valleys between them, with more than 7,000 m of climbing before Namche, and where the road has taken over the old path you walk on or beside a dusty track. From Namche on, it is the standard base camp trek with the standard altitude.

Highlights

  • Walking the pre-airstrip route to Everest from end to end
  • Rhododendron and fir forest on the Lamjura La
  • Junbesi and the Sherpa villages of Solu
  • A first distant sight of Everest from Phurteng, long before you reach it
  • Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar
  • Quiet lodges and very few trekkers until Chheplung

Challenging: The same altitude as the base camp trek, reached after nine days of steep hill walking with more than 7,000 m of climbing before Namche.

Ways to change it

Start at Shivalaya or Bhandar

Stay on the bus to Shivalaya and begin with day 3, or take a jeep to Bhandar in the dry season and begin with day 4. This is what most people now do, and it saves one or two days.

Join from Phaplu or Kharikhola

Jeeps from Kathmandu reach Salleri and Phaplu in a day and continue to Kharikhola or Thamdanda when the track is open. Starting there cuts the walk-in to between two and five days but misses the Lamjura La.

Add Pikey Peak

From Junbesi, take two days to climb to Jase Bhanjyang and the 4,065 m summit of Pikey Peak for a wide view of the Everest range, then return to Junbesi and carry on.

Walk out as well

Skip the Lukla flight and walk back down through Surke and Paiya to the road at Thamdanda or Kharikhola in two days, then take a jeep to Kathmandu by way of Salleri.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, and that covers the Gaurishankar and Solu part of this route. Checks on the trail are inconsistent, and Khumbu itself has not enforced the rule. On the walk-in a guide helps most where new jeep tracks have cut the old trail: the foot short-cuts are unsigned, and the right one is often not the obvious one.

Gaurishankar CA

Conservation area

Gaurishankar Conservation Area Permit · National Trust for Nature Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, single entry

Where: Online at NTNC's e-permit site or at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu. A permit issued at a trail checkpost costs double.

The fee includes tax and children under 10 do not need a permit. NTNC lists Rolwaling and Tsho Rolpa, Kalinchowk and the Shivalaya walk-in towards Everest under this permit. Upper Rolwaling needs the Dolakha restricted-area permit too.

Sagarmatha National Park

National park

Sagarmatha National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: At the park gate in Monjo, or in advance at the park counter in the Tourist Service Centre, Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

Children under 10 enter free. Nepali press reports from 2024 to 2026 give the same NPR 3,000, 1,500 and 100. Whether 13% VAT is added for foreigners is disputed: an independent guide says tax has been folded into park fees since 2019, while several agency sites still quote NPR 3,390. We found no official statement, so carry the difference in cash. Keep the permit handy; it is checked at the gate.

Khumbu municipality permit

Local permit

Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality entry permit · Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person
SAARC and Chinese nationalsNPR 2,000person

Where: At the municipality's tourist information centre in Lukla when you land, or at the Monjo checkpoint if you walk in from the road. It cannot be bought in Kathmandu.

The municipality calls it a Trek Card: staff take your passport details and a photo, which takes about 15 minutes. It has stood in for the TIMS card in Khumbu since October 2017, when the fee was NPR 2,000. The municipality's own page now lists NPR 3,000, with NPR 2,000 for SAARC and Chinese passport holders, and its 2024–25 visitor and revenue figures fit that split. A few agency pages say every foreigner pays NPR 3,000. Bring rupees in cash.

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

Buses for Jiri leave the old bus park at Ratna Park in Kathmandu early each morning and take seven to eight hours by way of Charikot. Several carry on to Shivalaya, and in the dry months jeeps go as far as Bhandar. You finish with the flight out of Lukla, which depends on the weather, so keep a spare day at the end.

  • Bus: Kathmandu to Jiri · 7–8 h

    About 190 km on a winding, mostly sealed road through Charikot. Morning departures only.

  • Bus: Kathmandu to Shivalaya · 9–10 h

    The same road, an hour or two further. This is where most trekkers now get off and start walking.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Bhandar · 9–11 h

    On a rough track beyond Shivalaya, in the dry season only. It saves two walking days.

  • Flight: Lukla to Kathmandu · 35 min

    The usual way home. In peak season most flights land at Manthali, four to five hours by road from Kathmandu.

Hazards on this route

Altitude above Namche

Walking in makes you fit, not acclimatized: the highest point before Namche is 3,530 m and you do not sleep there. Above Namche you face the same risks as people who flew to Lukla. Keep both rest days, and go down if symptoms get worse.

Jeep tracks and road building

Roads now cut the old trail between Jiri and Kinja and again from Ringmo to beyond Kharikhola. Expect dust, vehicles and, in places, diggers and landslide debris. Where the footpath and the track part, the footpath is usually the shorter and steeper line.

Mule trains south of Lukla

Between Kharikhola and Chheplung the trail is the supply line for Khumbu. Long mule trains pass on narrow ledges and churn the path to mud after rain. Stand on the uphill side and wait for the whole train to go by.

Heat low down, ice high up

The valley floors at Kinja and the Dudh Koshi bridge are hot from April to September, while the Lamjura La holds snow and ice from December to March. Carry enough water for the long climbs and light traction spikes in winter.

The flight out of Lukla

Cloud at Lukla cancels flights for a day or more at a time. Leave spare days before your international flight, or plan to walk back out to the road.

Practical tips

  • Bring all your cash from Kathmandu. Jiri has banks, but there is no dependable cash machine between there and Namche.
  • Lodges in Solu are family homes with a few rooms, cheaper and quieter than in Khumbu. Out of season some close, so ask each morning what is open ahead.
  • Between the Taksindu La and the Dudh Koshi you lose almost 1,600 m. Use two poles and take the descent slowly.
  • The Gaurishankar permit can be bought in Kathmandu or at the Shivalaya checkpost. The national park and Khumbu municipality permits are both issued at Monjo.
  • Start walking by seven. Mornings are clear and cool; by early afternoon the low valleys are hot and the passes are in cloud.
  • Heights quoted for the Solu villages vary by 100 m or more between maps, and the road-head moves every year. Ask in Kathmandu how far buses and jeeps are running before you fix your first day.

Quick answers

How long does the Jiri to Everest Base Camp trek take?

Most people take 17–23 days. The itinerary on this page is 20 days, including 2 days for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Jiri to Everest Base Camp trek go?

The highest point is Kala Patthar at 5,545 m (18,192 ft). The highest place you sleep is Gorak Shep at 5,164 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it challenging. The same altitude as the base camp trek, reached after nine days of steep hill walking with more than 7,000 m of climbing before Namche.

Which permits do I need?

Gaurishankar CA, Sagarmatha National Park, Khumbu municipality permit. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, and that covers the Gaurishankar and Solu part of this route. Checks on the trail are inconsistent, and Khumbu itself has not enforced the rule. On the walk-in a guide helps most where new jeep tracks have cut the old trail: the foot short-cuts are unsigned, and the right one is often not the obvious one.

When is the best time to go?

Mar–Apr, Oct–Nov are best. October and November are the clearest from start to finish. March and April bring rhododendron bloom on the Lamjura La, with haze and heat in the low valleys. In May and late September the lower trail is hot, wet in places and has leeches. From December to February the Solu hills are pleasant by day, but the Lamjura La can be icy and upper Khumbu is very cold.
