Trekking agencies

For restricted areas an agency is the law. Everywhere else a good one saves you days of logistics, and a bad one is the most expensive mistake you can make.

When you need one

  • Restricted areas. Manaslu, Tsum, Upper Mustang, Nar Phu, Dolpo, Kanchenjunga and the other restricted zones issue permits only through a registered agency, with a licensed guide.
  • TIMS cards. Outside Khumbu the trekker registration card is issued through agencies.
  • Camping routes. Dhaulagiri, Upper Dolpo and similar trips need tents, a kitchen crew and pack animals that only an outfitter can put together.

Questions worth asking

  • What are your company registration, tourism licence and tax numbers?
  • Who exactly will guide us, and can we speak to them before we pay?
  • How are your guides and porters insured, and to what altitude?
  • What is the porter load limit on your trips?
  • What is included, line by line, and what is not?
  • What happens to my money if flights are cancelled or I turn back early?

Warning signs

  • A price far below everyone else's. The saving comes out of staff wages, insurance or your safety margin.
  • Pressure to take a helicopter down for a mild illness. Unnecessary evacuations billed to insurers are a known fraud; call your insurer yourself before agreeing to any flight.
  • Itineraries that skip rest days to look shorter.
  • No written quote and no company papers.

What a legal agency holds

A registered company with a Department of Tourism trekking licence, PAN/VAT registration and a Nepal Rastra Bank licence to handle foreign currency; you can look it up in TAAN's member list

TAAN, the trekking agencies' association, asks a company for these papers before it can join: its company registration, the Department of Tourism's licence to run a trekking business, a PAN/VAT certificate and Nepal Rastra Bank's foreign exchange licence. Ask the agency for the numbers and look it up in TAAN's general members list, which showed 2,344 agencies on 22 November 2026. Nepal Rastra Bank also publishes the businesses it has licensed for foreign exchange. Only a registered agency can buy a TIMS card or apply for a restricted-area permit, so a company that cannot show its papers cannot legally arrange either.

Permit fees for every region are on the permits page. An agency charges those at cost plus a service fee; ask to see the breakdown.

How to get a price from a trekking company

Ask two or three companies for the same trek on the same dates, so the prices can be compared.

Ask with your plan

  1. Make your plan. A few simple questions set out your dates, your route and how many you are.
  2. Send it. Press Share or Print on the plan and send it to two or three registered trekking companies.
  3. Compare the answers. Look at what each price includes, line by line, and who will guide you.

Check before you pay

  • Look the company up. Find it in the trekking agencies' association member list.
  • Ask for its numbers. Company registration, tourism licence and tax (PAN) number. The names should match.
  • Get a written quote. What is included, what is not, and what happens to your money if a flight is cancelled.
  • Ask who will guide you. A good company lets you speak to your guide before you pay.

Do you run a registered agency?

List your company with its registration details, the routes you operate and your staff standards. We verify the papers before anything is published.
