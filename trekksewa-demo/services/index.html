Guides, porters and trekking companies

You do not have to do this alone. Here is who can help you on a trek in Nepal, what each of them does, and what they cost.

Which help do I need?

Many people take a company for their first trek, and a guide and porter after that.

A trekking company

Best for a first trek, a short holiday, or anywhere a permit needs an agency.

Arranges everything: guide, porter, permits, transport and beds. One price, one person to call if something goes wrong.

Usually a single price for the whole trek. Each trek page shows the range companies publish.

More about this

A guide

Best if you are happy to organise your own travel and permits but want someone who knows the way.

Walks with you, sets a safe pace, books the lodges, watches how you cope with the height and deals with any problem.

About USD 25–40 a day (roughly NPR 3,800–6,100); a few agencies quote up to USD 50

More about this

A porter

Best if you want to walk with only a small day bag. It makes every day easier, at any age.

Carries your main bag between lodges, up to about 20 to 25 kg, usually for two trekkers.

About USD 18–25 a day (roughly NPR 2,700–3,800); some agencies quote up to USD 30

Enrolling founding partners

Guides

Licensed trekking guides who know the route, the lodges and the signs of altitude illness.

Porters

The people who carry the loads. Hire them directly and fairly, with insurance and proper kit.

Agencies

Registered companies that arrange permits, staff, transport and full trips, and are required for restricted areas.

Gear

Shops in Kathmandu, Pokhara and on the trail that rent and sell what you need.

How to arrange help, step by step

Whoever you choose, the same four steps keep you safe.

  1. 1

    Make your plan

    Dates, route, how many of you and the help you want. The planner puts it on one page.

  2. 2

    Ask for a price

    Send the plan to two or three registered companies or guides and compare what each price includes.

  3. 3

    Check their papers

    A guide shows a licence card. A company shows its registration. Both should show staff insurance.

  4. 4

    Agree it in writing

    Dates, daily rate, what is included, the porter's load and what happens if you turn back early.

The fair-trek standards

What every partner agrees to

Based on the International Porter Protection Group's guidelines and on how the better Nepali agencies already work. Partners accept them when they apply.

Checked credentials

Guides are listed with their licence number and agencies with their registration, tourism licence and tax numbers, so you can verify them yourself.

Loads within limits

Porters carry no more than 25 kg including their own kit, and less at altitude or on rough ground.

Wages agreed and paid in full

The daily rate, what it includes and who pays for food and lodging are agreed before the trek and paid without deductions.

Everyone insured

Guides and porters are covered for accident, medical care and evacuation for the whole route, including its highest point.

Dressed for the altitude

Staff have boots, warm clothing, a sleeping place and food suited to the highest and coldest night of the trek.

Nobody sent down alone

A sick or injured guide or porter gets the same care as a client and is never paid off and sent down unaccompanied.

Do you work in trekking?

Guides, porters, agencies and gear shops can apply now. It takes about five minutes, in English or Nepali, and applying is free.
