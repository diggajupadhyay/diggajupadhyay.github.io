Altitude sickness check

Answer for how you feel right now. It takes a minute, works offline and tells you plainly whether to carry on, rest or go down.

This is an educational aid, not a diagnosis. It cannot examine you and it can be wrong. If someone is getting worse at altitude, go down. If you are unsure, go down.

    Stored only on this device. Scores that rise from one check to the next matter more than any single number.

    How this works

    The questions are the Lake Louise Acute Mountain Sickness score in its 2018 revision, the scale doctors and researchers use in the field. Four symptoms are each scored from 0 to 3. Altitude sickness is a headache with a total score of 3 or more, after a recent gain in height. A score of 3 to 5 is mild, 6 to 9 moderate and 10 to 12 severe.

    The danger signs are separate. They point to the two life-threatening forms of altitude illness, in the brain (HACE) and the lungs (HAPE), and any one of them means descending at once whatever the score says.

    Wait about six hours after arriving at a new height before you trust a low score: symptoms take time to appear. Repeat the check morning and evening while you are above 3,000 m.
