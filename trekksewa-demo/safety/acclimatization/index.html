Acclimatization checker

Load one of our itineraries or type in your own sleeping altitudes. The checker marks the nights that gain height too fast and shows what an extra night does.

What the checker looks for

  • Daily gain. Once you sleep above 3,000 m, a rise of more than about 500 m in sleeping altitude is marked for attention, and more than 800 m is marked as too fast.
  • Rest nights. Roughly every 1,000 m gained should include a night at the same height or lower.
  • The first jump. Arriving from below 1,500 m to sleep above 2,800 m in one day, by road or by air, is a known risk.

Only where you sleep counts. A day hike to a high viewpoint followed by a night lower down helps rather than hurts.

This is an educational aid, not a diagnosis. It cannot examine you and it can be wrong. If someone is getting worse at altitude, go down. If you are unsure, go down.

The thresholds follow the Wilderness Medical Society's practice guidelines for altitude illness. Many standard itineraries break them by a small margin, and most people cope. A marked night is a reason to pay attention, not proof of danger. Check your symptoms on the day.
