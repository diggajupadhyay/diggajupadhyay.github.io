Help and common questions

How the site works, what it can and cannot do offline, and how far to trust it.

Using the site

Is it free?

Yes. Reading the guides and using every tool is free and needs no account.

Do I need to sign up?

No. Saved treks, plans, packing lists and your emergency card are stored in your browser on your own device. Nothing is sent to us.

Will my saved things appear on my other phone or laptop?

No. Because they live in your browser, each device has its own. Clearing your browser data for this site removes them.

Is there an app?

TrekkSewa is a web app. On most phones you can add it to your home screen from the browser menu and it will open full-screen like any other app.

Offline

What works without a connection?

The safety tools (altitude check, acclimatization checker, emergency page), your saved treks, and every page you have already opened. Open the pages you will need while you still have Wi-Fi, or use the “Keep offline” button on a trek.

Do the maps work offline?

Map tiles you have already looked at are kept, so pan and zoom along your route beforehand. We do not bulk-download maps, because the free map services we use do not allow it. For full offline mapping carry a dedicated app or a paper map.

Does the forecast work offline?

No. You will see the last forecast you loaded, with its time, marked as saved.

Does GPS work without signal?

Yes. Your phone finds its position from satellites, not the mobile network. Sending that position to someone does need signal or Wi-Fi.

Accuracy

How accurate are the route guides?

They are written from long familiarity with these routes and checked against published sources, but lodges close, trails move and rules change. Each trek states how well established its details are. Treat distances and times as approximate and confirm anything important locally.

How accurate are the map markers?

Most markers take their position from OpenStreetMap; the rest are our own estimates and are labelled approximate. Places we could not confirm are not drawn at all. The dashed line on a trek map simply joins the stops in order. Follow the trail on the map and on the ground, not the line.

Are permit fees current?

They were last reviewed in November 2026, and each permit shows whether we could confirm it. Fees change, sometimes mid-season, so check with the Nepal Tourism Board or your agency before you pay.

Is the altitude sickness check a diagnosis?

No. It is an educational aid based on a standard scoring system. It cannot examine you. If someone is getting worse at altitude, descend.

Guides, porters, agencies and gear

Can I book a guide or porter through the site?

Not yet. We are enrolling partners now and will open the directory once there are enough verified profiles to be useful.

How do I get listed?

Fill in the application on the partners page. It takes about five minutes and can be completed in English or Nepali.

Does it cost anything to apply?

No.

Found a mistake?

If a stage, an altitude, a fee or a phone number is wrong, we want to know. Tell us which page, what is wrong and how you know.

A contact address for corrections will be published here when the partner network opens.

In an emergency

This page cannot help you in an emergency. Open the emergency toolkit, or call Nepal Police on 100 or the Tourist Police on 1144.
