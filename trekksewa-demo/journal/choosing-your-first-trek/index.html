Choosing your first trek in Nepal

Four questions that narrow dozens of routes down to two or three, and the mistakes first-timers make most often.

Nepal has more good treks than anyone has time for, and the famous names are not automatically the right ones for a first visit. Four questions do most of the choosing.

1. How many days do you really have?

Count the days between landing in Kathmandu and flying home, then take off three: one to arrive and sort permits, one to get back from the trail, and one spare for weather, a strike or a cancelled flight. What is left is your trekking time.

  • Up to five days: ridge treks close to Pokhara or Kathmandu. Poon Hill, Mardi Himal, Ama Yangri and the Kathmandu Valley rim all fit.
  • Six to ten days: Annapurna Base Camp, Langtang Valley, Gosaikunda, Khopra Ridge.
  • Eleven to fifteen days: Everest Base Camp, Gokyo, the Annapurna Circuit, Manaslu.
  • More than that: the three passes, Kanchenjunga, Makalu, Dolpo.

The single most common planning mistake is squeezing a twelve-day route into ten by deleting the rest days. Those days are how you get to the top.

2. How high are you prepared to sleep?

Fitness helps you enjoy a trek. It does not protect you from altitude, and very fit people often get into trouble because they can climb faster than their bodies can adapt.

If you have never slept above 3,000 m, you do not know yet how you respond. A sensible first trek tops out somewhere between 3,200 and 4,200 m, high enough to feel the mountains and low enough that a bad reaction is an uncomfortable night rather than an evacuation. Poon Hill, Mardi Himal, Langtang Valley and Annapurna Base Camp are all in that range.

Everest Base Camp is a perfectly reasonable first trek too, provided you keep both acclimatization days and are honest about symptoms. Every route on this site shows its sleeping altitudes night by night, and the acclimatization checker shows where the plan is tight.

3. When are you going?

Season matters more than route.

  • October and November have the clearest skies and the most people.
  • March to May is warmer, with rhododendrons in bloom and haze in the afternoons.
  • December to February is cold, clear and quiet. Stay below about 4,000 m unless you are equipped for snow.
  • June to September is the monsoon. The rain-shadow regions of Mustang and Dolpo are the places to go.

If your dates are fixed, pick the trek to suit the month, not the other way round. The trek filters let you do exactly that.

4. How much of the logistics do you want to handle?

Roughly three ways to go:

  • Fully organised through a registered agency: transport, permits, guide, porters and lodges arranged, at a price.
  • Guide and porter hired directly, with you booking transport and paying lodges yourself. Cheaper and more flexible.
  • Independent. Since 2023 the tourism board's rules have required foreign trekkers to take a licensed guide on most routes outside the Everest region, though checks on the trail are uneven. Restricted areas such as Manaslu and Upper Mustang always need an agency and a guide.

For a first trek, a guide is worth more than the fee suggests. They know which lodge has beds, when the weather is turning and when your headache is no longer something to walk off.

Three good first treks

Ghorepani Poon Hill, 4–5 days. Stone staircases, rhododendron forest and one of the great sunrise views, never above 3,210 m. The best answer when time is short.

Langtang Valley, 7–8 days. No flight needed, a real high valley at the end and a choice of viewpoints above 4,500 m as optional day walks.

Annapurna Base Camp, 8–10 days. Forest, villages and then a ring of 7,000 m peaks at 4,130 m. Longer days and many steps, but the altitude gain is steady.

And three mistakes to avoid

  1. Booking the flight home for the day after the trek. Leave at least one spare day, two if a mountain flight is involved.
  2. Buying new boots for the trip. Walk in them for weeks first.
  3. Treating the itinerary as a contract. The plan that gets you home is the one you are willing to change.
