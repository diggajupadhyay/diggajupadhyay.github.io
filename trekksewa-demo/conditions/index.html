Trail and trailhead conditions

What the season usually brings, a live forecast for the places where weather decides your day, and the roads, flights and passes that most often hold people up.

Current notices

Major damage we know about that changes how you reach or walk a route. This is not a complete list, and it is only as fresh as the date on each notice.

Langtang: trails reopened, road to Syabrubesi cut by the August 2026 flood

The Bhote Koshi flood of 26 August 2026 badly damaged Syabrubesi and swept away the road bridge on the Dhunche side. The road from Kathmandu reopened as far as Dhunche on 11 October over a temporary bridge at Betrawati; agencies say only jeeps and other light vehicles use it. Langtang, Kyanjin and Gosaikunda are open, reached on foot from Dhunche or Thulo Syabru by an alternative trail to Bamboo and Lama Hotel that opened at the end of October. The Tamang Heritage Trail reopened around 25 October over a temporary footbridge at Syabrubesi, but a second footbridge towards Briddim was still being built. A vehicle bridge at Syabrubesi was due before Tihar in early November, and we found no report that it has opened. A news briefing on 4 November still listed the Langtang and Gosaikunda routes as open.

What to do: Plan to start walking from Dhunche or Thulo Syabru and allow an extra day. Before you book transport, ask your agency or a lodge owner where vehicles stop this week, what kind of vehicle gets through and which footbridges are in use. For the Tamang Heritage Trail, also ask how to reach Syabrubesi and whether the Briddim side is open.

Latest report we found: 4 November 2026. We looked again on 22 November 2026 and found nothing newer.

Manaslu and Tsum: reopened on temporary bridges after the October 2026 flood

A flood on 11 October 2026 destroyed bridges and stretches of trail in the Budhi Gandaki gorge and in lower Tsum. The Manaslu Circuit reopened around 22 October: vehicles reach Machha Khola, and trekkers have been getting through Khorlabesi, Jagat and Philim, with a short bypass path at Yaru Bagar. The way into Tsum reopened on 25 October over four temporary wooden bridges, after the cantilever bridge at Sardibhir was lost. The municipality chair has said those bridges will not survive the next rains. A news briefing on 4 November also reported a rockfall blocking the side trail to Serang Gompa, which we could not confirm.

What to do: Ask your agency for the state of the gorge trail, the Yaru Bagar bypass and the Tsum bridges in the week you go. Expect slow, rough going on temporary paths and bridges, keep a spare day, and do not set out in or just after heavy rain.

Latest report we found: 4 November 2026. We looked again on 22 November 2026 and found nothing newer.

Roads and flights that cause delays

The usual pattern for each route in and out, so you know where to put your spare days. This is background, not today's status.

Lukla flights

Everest region

Small aircraft fly by sight into a short mountain runway, so cloud or wind at either end stops everything. Cancellations happen in every month and can last several days. In the busy spring and autumn weeks most flights leave from Manthali in Ramechhap, four to five hours' drive from Kathmandu.

Most trouble: Jun–Sep

Spare time to allow: One spare day in season, two or more in the monsoon and its shoulders. Never book an international flight for the day after you fly out.

Road to Salleri and Thamdanda

Everest region by road

The way in when you would rather not fly, or cannot. It is a long day to Salleri and rougher beyond, where the track towards Thamdanda is passable only by four-wheel drive and often not at all after rain.

Most trouble: Jun–Sep

Spare time to allow: Allow two full days to reach the trail and two to four extra walking days to Lukla.

Kathmandu to Syabrubesi

Langtang, Gosaikunda, Tamang Heritage Trail

The only road into Langtang climbs out of the Trishuli valley on a slope that slides in most monsoons. Expect seven to nine hours when it is clear, and sections where passengers walk across a slip to a vehicle waiting on the other side.

Most trouble: Jun–Sep

Spare time to allow: Half a day in the dry months, a full day when it has been raining.

Besisahar to Chame and Manang

Annapurna Circuit, Nar Phu, Tilicho

A rough jeep track cut into the Marsyangdi gorge. It shortens the circuit by days but closes with landslides in the monsoon and with snow and ice above Chame in winter. The ride is slow, dusty and hard on the back.

Most trouble: Jun–Sep

Spare time to allow: Treat any jeep time you are quoted as a minimum.

Beni to Jomsom

Lower and Upper Mustang, the end of the Annapurna Circuit

The Kali Gandaki road is open most of the year but is cut by rockfall and washouts in the wet months, mainly between Tatopani and Ghasa. Buses and jeeps take a long day from Pokhara.

Most trouble: Jun–Sep

Spare time to allow: A spare day if you are travelling by road in the monsoon.

Pokhara to Jomsom flights

Mustang

A twenty-minute flight through the deepest valley in the range. The wind that makes the Kali Gandaki famous rises by mid-morning, so flights go only in the first hours of the day and are cancelled readily.

Most trouble: Jan–Feb, Jun–Aug

Spare time to allow: Have the road as a fallback and a spare day before any onward connection.

Kathmandu to Soti Khola and Machha Khola

Manaslu Circuit, Tsum Valley

A full day by bus or jeep through Dhading and Arughat. The last section up the Budhi Gandaki is a narrow track below unstable slopes and is regularly blocked in the rains; the trail beyond it suffers the same.

Most trouble: Jun–Sep

Spare time to allow: A spare day in the wet months, and ask about the gorge trail before you set off.

Flights to Juphal, Jumla and Talcha

Dolpo, Rara

Short-runway mountain airstrips reached from Nepalgunj. Flights go early, in clear weather only, and a missed day pushes everyone back because there are few seats. Afternoon heat and cloud end flying well before noon.

Most trouble: Jun–Sep

Spare time to allow: Two spare days at the end of any trek that relies on these flights.

Roads to Taplejung and Num

Kanchenjunga, Makalu

After the flight to Bhadrapur or Tumlingtar the approach continues for many hours on hill roads that are rough at the best of times. The east gets the monsoon first and keeps it longest.

Most trouble: Jun–Sep

Spare time to allow: A spare day at each end of the trek.

High passes

When each pass is normally crossable. Fresh snow can close any of them for days inside the open season, and none has an official open or closed status.

PassAltitudeUsually crossableWhat stops people
Tashi Lapcha
Rolwaling		 5,755 m Apr–May, Oct–Nov A glaciated mountaineering pass between Rolwaling and Khumbu with rockfall and crevasses. Rope, crampons and a climbing guide are needed. It is not a trekking route.
Kongma La
Everest		 5,535 m Apr–May, Oct–Nov The highest and longest of the three Everest passes, with no lodge or water on the way and a rough moraine crossing to Lobuche at the end of the day.
Cho La
Everest		 5,420 m Mar–May, Oct–Nov A short glacier crossing on the east side that turns to hard ice early and late in the season. Do not attempt it after fresh snow, when the route and the crevasses are hidden.
Thorong La
Annapurna		 5,416 m Mar–May, Sep–Dec Deep snow from late December to February. Sudden storms happen even in peak season: one in October 2014 killed more than 40 people around the pass. Leave before dawn to be over before the wind.
Renjo La
Everest		 5,360 m Mar–May, Oct–Nov Stone steps on the Gokyo side hold ice, and there is no lodge between Gokyo and Lungden. A long day with no shelter if the weather turns.
French Pass
Annapurna		 5,360 m Apr–May, Oct–Nov Glacier travel with camping only. Between here and the Dhampus Pass there is no quick way down, so a storm in Hidden Valley is serious.
Kang La (Dolpo)
Western Nepal		 5,350 m May–Oct A remote camping pass on the way to Shey Gompa. The rain shadow keeps it open through the summer, but early snow can arrive from late October.
Kang La
Annapurna		 5,320 m Apr–May, Sep–Nov Snow lingers on the Nar side and the descent to Ngawal is steep and loose. A pre-dawn start from Nar is normal.
Dhampus Pass
Annapurna		 5,240 m Apr–May, Oct–Nov A long icy traverse high above the Kali Gandaki after the pass. Wind and whiteout are the usual problems.
Ganja La
Langtang		 5,122 m Apr–May, Oct–Nov Steep and exposed near the top and technical under snow. No lodges for several days: camping, a rope and a guide who knows the route.
Larkya La
Manaslu		 5,106 m Mar–May, Sep–Nov Usually snowbound from late December to early March. A very long day from Dharamsala with a steep, loose descent. Wind rises sharply after mid-morning.
Jeng La
Western Nepal		 5,090 m May–Oct The way out of Upper Dolpo towards Tokyu and Dho Tarap. Days from the nearest road, so turn back early if the weather is building.
Sinelapche La
Eastern Nepal		 4,646 m Apr–May, Oct–Nov The last of the linked passes between Ghunsa and Tseram on the Kanchenjunga circuit. A long day with no shelter and a route that is hard to follow in cloud or new snow.
Laurebina La
Langtang		 4,610 m Mar–May, Oct–Dec Snow from January to March hides the trail between Gosaikunda and Phedi, and trekkers have been lost here in cloud. Do not cross alone in poor visibility.
Shipton La
Eastern Nepal		 4,216 m Apr–May, Oct–Nov Snow lies late on the ridge between Khongma and Dobate, and in a heavy year these passes stay blocked into May.

Check on the day

These are the offices that issue forecasts, warnings and closures in Nepal. Look at them the evening before a road journey, a flight or a pass.

  • Meteorological Forecasting DivisionThe national weather service: daily forecasts, mountain forecasts and severe-weather warnings.
  • Flood forecasting, Department of Hydrology and MeteorologyRainfall and river levels in real time, with flood warnings by river basin.
  • BIPAD disaster information portalThe government's record of landslides, floods and other incidents as they are reported.
  • Department of RoadsHighway closures and reopening notices.
  • Nepal Tourism BoardTrekking notices and changes to permit rules.
  • Himalayan Rescue AssociationOpening dates for the altitude clinics at Pheriche and Manang.
