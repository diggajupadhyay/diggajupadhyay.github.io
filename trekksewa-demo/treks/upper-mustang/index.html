Upper Mustang

A loop from Jomsom up the Kali Gandaki to Lo Manthang and back by Lo Gekar and Dhakmar, crossing a string of passes around 4,000 m in dry, wind-scoured country behind the Himalaya.

Is this trek right for me?

  • The walking

    12 days in all. Most walking days are 5 to 6 hours on foot, and the longest is about 8 hours. Five to seven hour days with real climbs, usually below 4,500 m.

  • The height

    The highest night is at 3,820 m. You will notice the thinner air, but going slowly is usually enough.

  • Where you sleep

    In lodges every night: a simple twin room, a shared bathroom and hot meals in a warm dining room.

  • The help you need

    This is a restricted area. A registered trekking company must arrange your permit, and a licensed guide walks with you.

  • What it costs

    A guided trip with a trekking company usually costs USD 1,500 to 2,250 a person, going by the prices 10 companies published for 2026.

Overview

Upper Mustang is the old kingdom of Lo, a high, treeless valley north of the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges that stayed closed to foreigners until 1992. The trek starts at Jomsom, passes the checkpoint at Kagbeni and then climbs out of the Kali Gandaki onto a plateau of ochre and grey ridges, crossing one 3,800 to 4,000 m pass after another to reach the walled town of Lo Manthang on the sixth day.

A jeep road now runs the whole way from Jomsom to Lo Manthang and on to the Tibet border, and it has changed the walk. On the standard stages you share stretches of it with jeeps, motorbikes and their dust, most of all between Jomsom and Chele. Between the villages, older foot trails still cut across the ridges away from the road, and the return by Lo Gekar and Dhakmar avoids it almost completely. A guide who knows these lines makes a real difference. The road also lets you shorten the trip by riding a section, which matters now that the permit is charged by the day.

The walking itself is moderate: paths are broad, the highest pass is about 4,280 m and you sleep between 2,800 and 3,850 m. What wears people down is the dryness, the sun and the wind that blows up the valley every afternoon. Lodges are simple family houses with good kitchens. Mustang lies in the rain shadow, so it is at its best from late spring to autumn, including the monsoon months when most of Nepal is under cloud.

Highlights

  • The walled town of Lo Manthang and its 15th-century temple murals
  • Red, ochre and grey cliffs honeycombed with ancient cave dwellings
  • The quiet western trail by Lo Gekar monastery and the red crags of Dhakmar
  • The long mani wall at Ghami and the dzong and monastery of Tsarang
  • Views south to Nilgiri and the Annapurnas from the high passes
  • The Tiji festival in Lo Manthang, usually in May

Moderate: No day is steep or very high, but the stages are long, dry and exposed to strong afternoon wind, and you stay above 3,500 m for a week.

Ways to change it

Eastern return by Yara and Tangge

From Lo Manthang, cross the Kali Gandaki at Dhi and return along the east side through Yara, Luri Gompa, Tangge and Tetang, finishing over the Gyu La to Muktinath. It adds two to three days, stays far from the road and has very long, waterless stages with basic lodging.

Jeep one way

Riding between Jomsom or Chhusang and Lo Manthang in one direction cuts the trip to about eight days and saves three or four days of permit fees. If you drive up, sleep a night at Ghami or Tsarang on the way to let your body adjust.

Chungsi cave trail

Between Samar and Syangboche a foot trail drops into a canyon to the Chungsi cave shrine and climbs out again. It is harder than the ridge route but has no vehicles.

Out via Muktinath

From Chhusang, climb through Tetang and over the Gyu La (4,077 m) to Muktinath instead of walking the road to Jomsom. A long, dry day of seven to eight hours.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. Upper Mustang is a restricted area. The permit is issued only through a registered agency and you must walk with a licensed guide; the checkpoint at Kagbeni records every group. Since 22 March 2026 the Department of Immigration issues the permit to a single trekker as well, so you no longer need a partner to apply. A local guide also finds the foot trails that avoid the road and the key-holders for locked temples and caves.

Upper Mustang RAP

Restricted area

Upper Mustang restricted area permit · Department of Immigration

All foreign nationalsUSD 50person per day

Where: Department of Immigration in Kathmandu (the Pokhara immigration office also issues them), through a registered trekking agency only.

The old flat fee of USD 500 for ten days was scrapped by a cabinet decision in November 2025. The Department of Immigration's fee table now shows USD 50 a day with no minimum stay, so a ten-day trip costs the same and a shorter one costs less. Since 22 March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker, but you still need a registered agency and a licensed guide (at most seven trekkers per guide). The permit is checked at Kagbeni, and you need an ACAP as well.

ACAP

Conservation area

Annapurna Conservation Area Permit · National Trust for Nature Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, single entry

Where: Online at NTNC's e-permit site, or in person at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu, and the Nepal Tourism Board office in Pokhara. A permit issued at a trail checkpost costs double.

The fee includes tax and children under 10 do not need a permit. Paying online by card adds a 2.9% gateway charge. Covers every trek in the Annapurna range and Lower Mustang; Upper Mustang and Nar Phu need a restricted-area permit as well.

Getting there

Fly from Pokhara to Jomsom in about 20 minutes, or take a jeep or bus up the Kali Gandaki road via Beni and Tatopani. Flights leave early and are cancelled when wind or cloud builds, so keep a spare day or be ready to switch to the road.

  • Flight: Pokhara to Jomsom · 20–25 min

    Morning departures only. Cancellations are common in the monsoon and on windy days.

  • Jeep: Pokhara to Jomsom · 7–9 h

    Via Beni and Tatopani. The road is surfaced in parts and rough in others, and landslides can close it for hours in the monsoon.

  • Bus: Pokhara to Jomsom · 9–11 h

    Direct buses leave Pokhara early in the morning; cheaper and slower than a jeep.

  • Jeep: Jomsom to Lo Manthang · 6–9 h

    Shared and private jeeps run the full length of the valley on a rough road. Riding one way, usually the return, cuts three to four days from the trek.

Hazards on this route

Wind and dust

A strong southerly wind fills the Kali Gandaki valley from late morning until dusk, lifting grit from the riverbed and the road. Walk early, wear wrap-around glasses and a buff, and expect chapped lips and a dry cough if you do not.

Vehicles on the road

Jeeps, motorbikes and tractors use the same road you walk on for part of most days. Step well off the track when you hear one, especially on blind corners and canyon sections, and take the foot trail wherever your guide offers one.

Altitude

Sleeping heights are moderate, but you stay above 3,500 m for a week and cross 4,000 m several times. Headaches on the second and third nights are common. If you ride a jeep north, break the journey for a night on the way; driving from Jomsom to Lo Manthang in one go gains 1,100 m in a day.

Sun and dehydration

The air is very dry and there is almost no shade between villages. Water sources are far apart on the passes; carry two litres from each village and treat what you refill.

Flash floods in the monsoon

Side streams and the Kali Gandaki can rise fast after rain higher up, as the flood that hit Kagbeni in August 2023 showed. In July and August, stay off the riverbed route when it has been raining and ask about the state of the road before you set out.

Practical tips

  • The restricted-area permit is charged at USD 50 per person per day; the older flat ten-day fee no longer applies. Count the days with your agency before they apply: the clock runs from the Kagbeni checkpoint until you pass it again, ten days on this plan.
  • Bring all your cash from Pokhara or Jomsom. There are no dependable cash machines north of Jomsom.
  • Start walking by 7 am and aim to arrive by early afternoon, before the wind is at its worst.
  • Temples in Lo Manthang, Tsarang and Lo Gekar charge entry fees and do not allow photographs inside; carry small notes.
  • Pass names and heights differ between maps, and the points where the foot trail leaves the road change as the road is rebuilt, so treat the heights and walking times here as approximate.
  • For the Tiji festival, usually in May, book lodges in Lo Manthang months ahead. The dates follow the Tibetan calendar and change every year.

Cash machines on the route: Jomsom. They run out of cash and go offline, so carry what you need from Pokhara. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Upper Mustang trek take?

Most people take 8–16 days. The itinerary on this page is 12 days, including 1 day for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Upper Mustang trek go?

The highest point is Chogo La at 4,280 m (14,042 ft). The highest place you sleep is Dhakmar at 3,820 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. No day is steep or very high, but the stages are long, dry and exposed to strong afternoon wind, and you stay above 3,500 m for a week.
