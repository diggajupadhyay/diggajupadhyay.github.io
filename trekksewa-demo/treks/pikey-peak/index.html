Pikey Peak

A road-access teahouse trek along the ridges of lower Solu to the 4,065 m summit of Pikey Peak, then down to the Sherpa village of Junbesi and out from Phaplu.

Is this trek right for me?

  • The walking

    7 days in all. Most walking days are 4 to 5 hours on foot, and the longest is about 8 hours. Five to seven hour days with real climbs, usually below 4,500 m.

  • The height

    The highest night is at 3,640 m. You will notice the thinner air, but going slowly is usually enough.

  • Where you sleep

    In lodges every night: a simple twin room, a shared bathroom and hot meals in a warm dining room.

  • The help you need

    You may walk without a guide here, but we strongly recommend one, and a porter if you would rather not carry a full pack.

  • What it costs

    A guided trip with a trekking company usually costs USD 450 to 900 a person, going by the prices 10 companies published for 2026.

Overview

Pikey Peak is the highest hill in lower Solu, the farmed Sherpa country south of the Everest national park. Its grassy summit stands clear of everything around it, so the sunrise view covers a long arc of the Himalaya: Gaurishankar and the Numbur range close at hand, the Everest group to the north-east and, when the air is clean, Makalu and Kanchenjunga beyond. The peak is named for a Sherpa clan deity, and prayer flags and cairns cover both of its tops.

The usual route starts from the road at Dhap, follows the ridge north for two days through pine and rhododendron forest, sleeps below the summit and comes down to Junbesi, one of the best-kept villages in Solu, before finishing at Phaplu. There is no flight to depend on and no national park fee, and you will meet far fewer trekkers than in Khumbu. In March and April the rhododendrons flower along most of the ridge.

It is a moderate trek, but not a stroll. A jeep lifts you from 1,400 m to about 2,850 m on the first day and you sleep at 3,640 m on the third, so mild altitude symptoms are common at the base camp. Lodges are family-run and simple, the ridge can hold snow from December to March, and the first two days share a dusty jeep track for long stretches.

Highlights

  • Sunrise from 4,065 m over Everest, Makalu, Numbur and Gaurishankar
  • Rhododendron and fir forest along the ridge, in flower in March and April
  • Mani walls, chortens and small monasteries on the approach
  • Junbesi village and the monastery at Thupten Chholing
  • No flight needed and very few other trekkers

Moderate: Ridge trails and jeep track at moderate distances, with one 800 m gain to sleep at 3,640 m and a pre-dawn climb to 4,065 m.

Ways to change it

Six days

Leave out the rest day in Junbesi and walk straight on to Phaplu.

A night at Jase Bhanjyang or Loding

Stop at the saddle after the summit, or continue down to the hamlet of Loding, to break the long descent to Junbesi into two easier days.

In reverse from Phaplu

Walking from Phaplu through Junbesi to Jase Bhanjyang raises your sleeping height more gently: 2,413 m, 2,700 m, then 3,520 m. You climb the peak from the north and finish with the ridge walk to Dhap.

Out over the Taksindu La

From Junbesi follow the old Jiri trail east to the Taksindu La and Nunthala and take a jeep from there. It adds a day and a first look at the route into Khumbu.

Permits and guide rules

Guide strongly recommended. Pikey lies outside the national parks and conservation areas, and nobody checks for a guide on this ridge. On paper the TIMS card for the route is tied to the Nepal Tourism Board rule of April 2023 that foreign trekkers walk with a licensed guide; in practice many people go independently. A guide earns the fee where the footpath keeps leaving and rejoining the jeep track above Jhapre, in the fog that settles on the ridge most afternoons, and by phoning ahead to lodges that open only when someone is expected.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.

Agency required

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

Dhap lies on the road from Okhaldhunga to Salleri, a full day's drive from Kathmandu. Shared jeeps bound for Salleri leave the capital before dawn and will drop you there. The trek ends at Phaplu, where jeeps leave for Kathmandu early each morning and a flight operates on some days.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Dhap · 8–9 h

    Shared jeeps, and a slower bus, follow the BP Highway to Khurkot, the Sun Koshi to Ghurmi and the hill road through Okhaldhunga. Book a seat the day before.

  • Jeep: Phaplu to Kathmandu · 9–10 h

    The way home, on the same road. Jeeps leave Phaplu and Salleri at about five in the morning.

  • Flight: Phaplu to Kathmandu · 30 min

    Flights are few and often cancelled. Check what is operating before you count on one.

Hazards on this route

Altitude at the base camp

You go from Kathmandu to a bed at 3,640 m in three days, the first of them by jeep. Headache, poor sleep and loss of appetite are common at the base camp. If the symptoms are more than mild, leave out the summit and go down to Jhapre or on to Junbesi.

Cold, wind and snow on the ridge

The summit ridge is exposed. Before dawn in autumn it is well below freezing with a strong wind, and from December to March snow can cover the trail above 3,300 m. Carry a warm jacket, hat and gloves whatever the season.

Fog and unmarked junctions

Cloud rises onto the ridge most afternoons, and the trail crosses jeep tracks and herders' paths with few signs. Do your walking in the morning, keep the group together and ask at each lodge about the junctions ahead.

The road journey

The drive is eight to ten hours each way on mountain roads with long rough sections. Use a reputable jeep operator, avoid travelling after dark and expect delays from landslides in the monsoon months.

Practical tips

  • Carry cash for the whole trek. The only cash machines are in Salleri, close to the end.
  • Take a filter or purification tablets and carry two litres on the ridge days. In the dry months there are few springs between Jhapre and the base camp.
  • Lodges at the base camp are small. In October, November and April have someone phone ahead from Jhapre.
  • Pikey is outside the national park, so there is no park fee. The TIMS card is the only paperwork and nothing on the route checks it; an agency arranges it if you go with a guide.
  • Bring a headlamp and a proper down jacket for the summit morning, even if the days feel warm.
  • Stage plans for Pikey run from five to nine days, and the heights quoted for Dhap, Jhapre and Jase Bhanjyang differ by up to 100 m between maps. Use the figures here as a guide.

We know of no cash machines on this route. Carry all the rupees you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Pikey Peak trek take?

Most people take 5–9 days. The itinerary on this page is 7 days, including 1 day for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Pikey Peak trek go?

The highest point is Pikey Peak at 4,065 m (13,337 ft). The highest place you sleep is Pikey Base Camp at 3,640 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. Ridge trails and jeep track at moderate distances, with one 800 m gain to sleep at 3,640 m and a pre-dawn climb to 4,065 m.
