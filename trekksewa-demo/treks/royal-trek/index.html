Annapurna Easy

Royal Trek

A low, quiet village walk east of Pokhara: three hilltop nights at Kalikasthan, Shaklung and Chisapani, never above about 1,400 m, ending on the shore of Begnas Lake.

Is this trek right for me?

  • The walking

    4 days in all. Most walking days are 4 to 5 hours on foot, and the longest is about 6 hours. Short days below 3,500 m on good paths. Fine for a first trek.

  • The height

    You never sleep above 1,380 m, so altitude is not a worry on this trek.

  • Where you sleep

    In lodges every night: a simple twin room, a shared bathroom and hot meals in a warm dining room.

  • The help you need

    A guide is optional here. Many people still take one for company, local knowledge and peace of mind.

Overview

The Royal Trek takes its name from Prince Charles, now King Charles III, who walked it with a large party in the early 1980s. It follows a line of low ridges east of Pokhara through Brahmin, Chhetri and Gurung farming villages, and comes down to Begnas Lake four days later. On a clear morning the whole range stands to the north, from Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre round to Lamjung Himal and Manaslu. You see it from a distance, though: this is a walk through farmland, not into the mountains.

It is about as low and gentle as trekking in Nepal gets. The hills top out at about 1,400 m, so altitude plays no part, and it can be walked in the middle of winter. What it does ask for is steady legs for four to six hours a day on village paths, stone steps and stretches of dirt road, with one sharp descent and climb on the third day. You are unlikely to meet another trekking group.

Be clear about two things before you book. First, this used to be a camping trek, and the places you sleep now are family homestays and a few simple lodges, with shared toilets and whatever the house is cooking. Someone has to phone ahead. Second, most printed itineraries still carry old altitudes, 1,730 m for Shaklung and 1,550 m for Chisapani, which terrain maps do not bear out; the heights given here are read from the map. Dirt roads now reach most of the villages, which takes away some of the quiet and makes it easy to cut a day short. Brochures give Shaklung as 1,730 m and Chisapani as about 1,550 m. The villages as mapped sit near 1,380 m and 1,300 m: the heights used here.

Highlights

  • The Annapurna range, Machhapuchhre and Manaslu from three hilltop villages
  • Brahmin, Chhetri and Gurung farming country with hardly another trekker
  • Begnas and Rupa lakes from the ridge above, then from the shore
  • Warm enough to walk in December and January
  • Starts and ends within an hour of Pokhara

Ways to change it

Three days

Ride to Kalikasthan by jeep and begin with the walk to Shaklung, or walk the first three days and take a jeep down from Chisapani.

A night by the lake

Stay at Sundari Danda or beside Begnas Lake on the last night instead of driving straight back to Pokhara. Both have guesthouses, and the lake is at its best early in the morning.

In the other direction

Starting at Begnas Lake puts the steepest climbing into the first two days and finishes with the easy ridge down to Bijayapur. The views are the same either way.

Permits and guide rules

Guide optional. The Royal Trek runs through farmland along the southern edge of the Annapurna Conservation Area, outside it as far as we can tell, and no permit post on the route checks for a guide. A guide is worth having all the same: the route is a web of village paths and farm roads with no signs for trekkers, and the homestays need a phone call in Nepali before you turn up.

No trekking permit is needed for this route.

Getting there

Both ends are close to Pokhara. The walk starts where the Prithvi Highway crosses the Bijayapur Khola, twenty to thirty minutes east of Lakeside by taxi, and ends at the bus stand below the dam at Begnas Lake, 30 to 45 minutes from the city. Kalikasthan and most of the other villages are on dirt roads, so a jeep can bring you in or out part-way.

  • Drive: Pokhara to Bijayapur · 20–30 min

    Taxi from Lakeside. City buses towards Begnas and Talchowk also pass the bridge.

  • Bus: Begnas Lake to Pokhara · 45 min–1 hr

    Local buses leave the stand below the dam all day for the centre of Pokhara. A taxi takes about half an hour.

  • Jeep: Pokhara to Kalikasthan · about 1 hr

    For a three-day version: ride up the ridge road and begin with the walk to Shaklung.

Hazards on this route

Losing the way

Village paths, farm tracks and new dirt roads cross each other all along these ridges, and nothing is signed for trekkers. Old descriptions no longer match the ground. Without a guide, ask the way at every village and carry an offline map with the four overnight places marked.

Heat and sun

At 800 to 1,400 m the ridges are warm even in winter and hot from March on, with long stretches without shade. Walk early, wear a hat and carry at least two litres of water each.

Steep, slippery descents

The drop from Shaklung on day three and the stone steps down to Rupa Lake on day four are steep, and the red clay is like soap after rain. Use two poles and do not hurry.

Very simple lodging

Rooms are in family houses: a hard bed, a squat toilet outside, a bucket wash and the food the family eats. If your stomach is easily upset, keep to freshly cooked dal bhat and treat all drinking water.

Practical tips

  • Have your guide or agency phone each homestay a day ahead. Outside the busy months the family may be in the fields or away in Pokhara.
  • Start walking by eight. The ridges have little shade, and the mountains are sharpest before the haze builds.
  • Carry water and something for lunch each day; tea shops are few between the villages.
  • Agencies routinely add an ACAP permit and a TIMS card to this trek. As far as we can map it, the route stays in the outer wards of Pokhara and in Rupa Rural Municipality, south of the conservation area, and it has no checkpost. We found no official ruling either way, so ask what the charge is for.
  • Bring a sleeping bag liner, a torch and small notes. Nobody on the route can change a large one.
  • Keep an afternoon for Begnas Lake at the end. Boats can be hired by the dam, and it is far quieter than Lakeside.

We know of no cash machines on this route. Carry all the rupees you need from Pokhara. Work out how much.

How long does the Royal Trek trek take?

Most people take 3–5 days. The itinerary on this page is 4 days.

How high does the Royal Trek trek go?
