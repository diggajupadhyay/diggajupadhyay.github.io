Ama Dablam Base Camp

A nine-day trek up the Everest trail to Pangboche, with a day walk to the expedition base camp below Ama Dablam at 4,570 m and no night above 4,000 m.

Is this trek right for me?

  • The walking

    9 days in all. Most walking days are 5 to 6 hours on foot, and the longest is about 7 hours. Five to seven hour days with real climbs, usually below 4,500 m.

  • The height

    The highest night is at 3,985 m. You will notice the thinner air, but going slowly is usually enough.

  • Where you sleep

    In lodges every night: a simple twin room, a shared bathroom and hot meals in a warm dining room.

  • The help you need

    You may walk without a guide here, but we strongly recommend one, and a porter if you would rather not carry a full pack.

  • What it costs

    A guided trip with a trekking company usually costs USD 1,050 to 1,450 a person, going by the prices 9 companies published for 2026.

Overview

Ama Dablam (6,812 m) is the peak you look at most on the Everest trail: a steep pyramid with two long ridges like arms and a hanging glacier high on its face. Sherpas liken that glacier to the dablam, the charm box a mother wears round her neck, and the mountain takes its name from it. The trek follows the main trail for four days, then leaves it at Pangboche, crosses the Imja Khola and climbs to the meadow where climbing expeditions set up their base camp.

Because you sleep in Pangboche at 3,985 m and visit 4,570 m only for a few hours, the altitude load is much lighter than on the Everest Base Camp trek, yet you end up directly under a big Himalayan face. In October and November, the main climbing season on Ama Dablam, the meadow is a working camp with kitchen tents and prayer flags and climbers coming and going. In spring and winter you may have it to yourself.

The base camp day follows a herders' path with no signs and no lodges, a change after the busy main trail. It is also the only strenuous day: you drop to the river first and then climb more than 600 m. The same walk fits into the Everest Base Camp trek as a side trip from Pangboche, either for extra acclimatization on the way up or as a last outing on the way down.

Highlights

  • Standing under the west face of Ama Dablam at 4,570 m
  • A working expedition base camp in October and November
  • Tengboche monastery and the old gompa in upper Pangboche
  • Everest and Lhotse from the ridge above Namche
  • No night above 4,000 m

Moderate: One strenuous day to 4,570 m; otherwise the well-graded Everest trail, with every night below 4,000 m.

Ways to change it

As a side trip from the Everest Base Camp trek

Add one day at Pangboche on the way up, which also helps acclimatization, or on the way down from Pheriche. The walk and the timings are the same.

Return through Phortse

From upper Pangboche a high trail contours to Phortse in two to three hours, with a fair chance of seeing Himalayan tahr. Continue over Mong La to Namche the same day or sleep in Phortse and add a day.

Eight days

Walk from Namche to Pangboche in one stage of six to seven hours and save the night at Tengboche. Do this only if you felt well at Namche.

Permits and guide rules

Guide strongly recommended. Khumbu has not enforced the national guide requirement, and on the main trail you will not lose your way. The path from Pangboche to base camp is different: it is unsigned and splits into yak tracks on the upper pasture, and in cloud it is easy to drift into the wrong valley. A licensed guide who has been up before is worth having for that day alone.

Sagarmatha National Park

National park

Sagarmatha National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: At the park gate in Monjo, or in advance at the park counter in the Tourist Service Centre, Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

Children under 10 enter free. Nepali press reports from 2024 to 2026 give the same NPR 3,000, 1,500 and 100. Whether 13% VAT is added for foreigners is disputed: an independent guide says tax has been folded into park fees since 2019, while several agency sites still quote NPR 3,390. We found no official statement, so carry the difference in cash. Keep the permit handy; it is checked at the gate.

Khumbu municipality permit

Local permit

Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality entry permit · Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person
SAARC and Chinese nationalsNPR 2,000person

Where: At the municipality's tourist information centre in Lukla when you land, or at the Monjo checkpoint if you walk in from the road. It cannot be bought in Kathmandu.

The municipality calls it a Trek Card: staff take your passport details and a photo, which takes about 15 minutes. It has stood in for the TIMS card in Khumbu since October 2017, when the fee was NPR 2,000. The municipality's own page now lists NPR 3,000, with NPR 2,000 for SAARC and Chinese passport holders, and its 2024–25 visitor and revenue figures fit that split. A few agency pages say every foreigner pays NPR 3,000. Bring rupees in cash.

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

The trek begins and ends with the flight to Lukla, which leaves in the early morning from Kathmandu or, in peak season, from Manthali in Ramechhap. Flights wait on the weather at Lukla, and a day's delay at either end is normal enough to plan for.

  • Flight: Kathmandu to Lukla · 35 min

    Direct flights run mostly outside the busiest weeks of spring and autumn.

  • Flight: Manthali to Lukla · 20 min

    The peak-season departure point, four to five hours' drive from Kathmandu; most people travel there overnight.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Lukla · 1–2 days + 2 days' walk

    Jeep to Salleri and on to Thamdanda when the track is open, then walk through Paiya and Surke. A fallback for long flight shutdowns.

Hazards on this route

Altitude on the base camp day

Going from a bed at 3,985 m to 4,570 m and back in a day is within normal limits, but you will feel it. A headache or nausea at base camp is a signal to start down, not to sit and wait for it to pass.

An unmarked path

There are no signs, lodges or passing trekkers on the far side of the river. Cloud often rolls up the valley after midday and the yak tracks look alike. Leave Pangboche early, note the landmarks on the way up and do not go alone.

Lukla flights

Flights are cancelled whenever cloud closes the approach to Lukla, sometimes for days. Keep at least one spare day before your international flight.

Practical tips

  • Order a packed lunch in Pangboche the night before; there is nothing dependable to eat on the way to base camp.
  • Expedition camps are workplaces. Ask before you walk between the tents or take photographs, and do not expect to be fed.
  • Upper Pangboche is a short climb above the lodges on the main trail and has the oldest monastery in Khumbu.
  • Bring the cash you need from Kathmandu or Namche; there is none to be had higher up.
  • If cloud hides the mountain in the morning and you have a spare day, wait for the next one. The walk is about what you see at the end.

Cash machines on the route: Lukla, Namche Bazaar. They run out of cash and go offline, so carry what you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Ama Dablam Base Camp trek take?

Most people take 8–11 days. The itinerary on this page is 9 days, including 1 day for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Ama Dablam Base Camp trek go?

The highest point is Ama Dablam Base Camp at 4,570 m (14,993 ft). The highest place you sleep is Pangboche at 3,985 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. One strenuous day to 4,570 m; otherwise the well-graded Everest trail, with every night below 4,000 m.

Which permits do I need?

Sagarmatha National Park, Khumbu municipality permit. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

Khumbu has not enforced the national guide requirement, and on the main trail you will not lose your way. The path from Pangboche to base camp is different: it is unsigned and splits into yak tracks on the upper pasture, and in cloud it is easy to drift into the wrong valley. A licensed guide who has been up before is worth having for that day alone.

When is the best time to go?

Mar–May, Oct–Nov are best. October and November combine the clearest weather with a busy base camp. Spring is warmer and hazier, and the meadow is usually empty. Winter is workable with warm gear, though snow can lie on the base camp path and fewer lodges are open in Pangboche. In the monsoon the mountain is hidden most days.
