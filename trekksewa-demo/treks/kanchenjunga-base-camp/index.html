Kanchenjunga Base Camp

A three-week route in Nepal's far east: up the Ghunsa valley to Pangpema below Kanchenjunga's north face, across the Sele La and Mirgin La, then to Oktang on the south side.

Is this trek right for me?

  • The walking

    19 days in all. Most walking days are 5 to 7 hours on foot, and the longest is about 9 hours. Remote, long and high, often with camping, snow or glacier travel.

  • The height

    You sleep as high as 4,780 m. Most people feel the altitude at this height. The plan includes the rest days you need: do not shorten it.

  • Where you sleep

    In lodges every night: a simple twin room, a shared bathroom and hot meals in a warm dining room.

  • The help you need

    This is a restricted area. A registered trekking company must arrange your permit, and a licensed guide walks with you.

  • What it costs

    A guided trip with a trekking company usually costs USD 1,600 to 2,500 a person, going by the prices 7 companies published for 2026.

Overview

Kanchenjunga, 8,586 m, stands on the border with Sikkim at the eastern end of Nepal. This route visits both sides of it. You walk up the Ghunsa Khola through Sherpa and Tibetan villages to Pangpema, the north base camp at 5,143 m, come back down to Ghunsa, cross a line of passes between about 4,300 and 4,650 m to the Simbuwa Khola, and climb again to the Oktang viewpoint above the Yalung Glacier.

Nothing here is technical, but it is a long trek in a place where help is far away. Count on about fourteen walking days, most of them six hours or more, with steep, rough trail in the lower gorges and loose moraine higher up. Lodges now cover every stage, yet above Ghunsa and on the passes they are plain stone or timber huts with a few beds, a stove and a short menu, and they shut when their owners go down for the winter or the monsoon.

The whole area is a restricted zone inside the Kanchenjunga Conservation Area, so the permit has to be arranged through a registered agency and you must walk with a licensed guide. Jeep tracks have shortened both ends of the trek in recent years, and how far vehicles get changes with every monsoon. Treat the first and last days of any plan as provisional and keep a spare day or two in hand.

Highlights

  • Pangpema and the north face of Kanchenjunga
  • Jannu's north wall from the meadows at Kambachen
  • Ghunsa, a Sherpa village of timber houses and prayer flags
  • The high traverse over Sinion La, Mirgin La and Sinelapche La
  • Oktang and the Yalung Glacier on the south side
  • Far fewer trekkers than on any route in Khumbu or Annapurna

Strenuous: Two weeks of long days on rough trail, two nights near 4,800 m, a day to 5,143 m and a full day of passes, all far from a road or a hospital.

Ways to change it

North base camp only

Walk in and out by the Ghunsa valley to Pangpema, about 14 to 16 days from Kathmandu. It skips the passes and the south side.

South base camp only

Yamphudin, Tortong, Cheram and Oktang and back the same way takes 11 to 14 days. A good choice when the passes are under snow.

South to north

The circuit works in either direction. Starting from Yamphudin gains height much faster on the way to Ramche, so most people go north first.

Walking the whole way

Before the jeep tracks, the trek began and ended at Taplejung or the Suketar airstrip above it. Doing so adds four to six days through farmland and cardamom plantations at each end of the loop.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. This is a restricted area: the permit is issued only through a registered trekking agency and you must be accompanied by a licensed guide, whether you come alone or in a group. A guide who knows the route also matters on the long pass day between the Sele La and Cheram, where the trail is faint in snow or cloud and there is no shelter.

Kanchenjunga RAP

Restricted area

Kanchenjunga restricted area permit · Department of Immigration

All foreign nationalsUSD 20person per week for the first four weeks, then USD 25 a week

Where: Department of Immigration in Kathmandu, through a registered trekking agency only.

Since 22 March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker; before that the minimum was two. You still have to book through a registered agency and walk with a licensed guide, with at most seven trekkers per guide. Covers Phaktanglung, Mikwakhola and Sirijunga rural municipalities in Taplejung. The conservation area permit is extra.

Agency required

Kanchenjunga CA

Conservation area

Kanchenjunga Conservation Area Permit · Kanchenjunga Conservation Area Management Council

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu, normally arranged by your agency with the restricted-area permit. Foreign trekkers are also registered at check posts on the trail, for example at Ghunsa.

NPR 2,000 for foreigners, NPR 500 for SAARC nationals and NPR 100 for Nepali citizens are the figures in the Nepal Tourism Board's current fee table; children under 10 are free. A few agency pages list NPR 3,000, which is the NTNC rate and does not apply here, because this area is run by a local council under the Department of National Parks.

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

Fly from Kathmandu to Bhadrapur in the south-eastern Terai, then drive north through the tea country of Ilam to Taplejung, usually over two days. From Taplejung a rough jeep track follows the Tamor river to Sekathum. Coming out, jeeps meet groups at Yamphudin when the track is open.

  • Flight: Kathmandu to Bhadrapur · 45 min

    Several flights a day; the usual way to start.

  • Drive: Bhadrapur to Taplejung · 9–10 hours

    A paved but slow hill road. Most groups break the journey in Ilam.

  • Jeep: Taplejung to Sekathum · 4–6 hours

    A rough track that landslides often cut in the wet months. If it is closed you start walking lower down, which can add two or three days.

  • Bus: Kathmandu to Taplejung · 20 hours or more

    Long-distance night buses run by way of Birtamod and Ilam. A hard start to a long trek; most people fly the first leg.

Hazards on this route

Altitude

You sleep at about 4,780 m at Lhonak and walk to 5,143 m the next day. Keep both acclimatization days, and if symptoms appear at Kambachen or Lhonak go down to Ghunsa, which has the only health post in the upper valley, and a basic one.

Remoteness and evacuation

A helicopter is the only fast way out, and it needs clear weather and a way to call it. Carry insurance that covers evacuation from above 5,000 m and agree with your guide how a call for help would be made from each stage.

Landslides and rockfall

The slope between Ghunsa and Kambachen, the moraine edge above Lhonak and the slide near Lasiya Bhanjyang all shed rock, especially after rain and in the afternoon thaw. Cross early, spaced out and without stopping.

The pass day

The Sele La to Cheram is seven hours or more over exposed ground with no shelter and little water. Fresh snow or cloud hides the trail. Do not set out late, alone or in bad weather.

Road travel

The drives to and from the trailheads are long, on narrow hill roads and rough tracks that slide in the monsoon. Travel by day and expect delays.

Practical tips

  • Bring all the cash you need in rupees from Kathmandu, Birtamod or Taplejung; there is nowhere to withdraw money after that.
  • A warm sleeping bag is essential. Lodges above Ghunsa give you a bed and, at best, a blanket.
  • Carry water treatment and a spare way to charge a phone and headlamp. Solar charging at the high lodges is limited and costs extra.
  • Before leaving Ghunsa and Cheram, have your guide confirm which huts at Lhonak, the Sele La and Ramche are open, particularly early and late in the season.
  • Count the days on your restricted-area permit carefully. It is charged by the week, and a weather delay can push you into the next one.
  • Outside the dry months, pack for leeches in the lower forest: long socks and salt or repellent.

Cash machines on the route: Bhadrapur, Ilam, Taplejung. They run out of cash and go offline, so carry what you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

How long does the Kanchenjunga Base Camp trek take?

Most people take 18–25 days. The itinerary on this page is 19 days, including 2 days for rest and acclimatization.
