Panch Pokhari

Nine days from Kathmandu: north along the ridge above Chautara by forest camps and herders' pastures to the five lakes of Panch Pokhari at about 4,100 m, then down to the road at Chhimti.

Is this trek right for me?

  • The walking

    9 days in all. Most walking days are 4 to 6 hours on foot, and the longest is about 7 hours. Long days and several nights above 4,000 m, or a pass above 5,000 m.

  • The height

    You sleep as high as 4,100 m. Most people feel the altitude at this height. The plan includes the rest days you need: do not shorten it.

  • Where you sleep

    In lodges on most nights, and in tents on a few: a simple twin room, a shared bathroom and hot meals in a warm dining room.

  • The help you need

    The rules require a licensed guide on this route. A porter to carry your bag is your choice.

Overview

Panch Pokhari means five ponds: a cluster of small lakes in a grassy bowl at about 4,100 m, in the Sindhupalchok corner of Langtang National Park. They are sacred to Shiva, and at the Janai Purnima full moon in August thousands of pilgrims climb through the rain to bathe here. For the rest of the year the place is very quiet. From the rim above the water the Jugal peaks, with Dorje Lakpa at their western end, fill the skyline to the north.

This guide follows the old approach from Chautara, the district town. For six days you walk north along a single ridge, from terraced farms through oak and rhododendron forest to yak pastures and open ground. Height comes slowly, never more than about 400 m of sleeping altitude a day above Kami Kharka, which makes this long way round kinder to the body than the short route from Bhotang, where people reach the lakes on the third day. The price is a ridge with almost no villages, few other trekkers and water points that are far apart.

Be clear about lodging. Chautara has hotels, and simple teahouses open in the trekking seasons at Nasim Pati and Tupi Danda on the Bhotang side. On the ridge between Sano Okhareni and Hille Bhanjyang there is nothing you can count on, and agencies run that section as a camping trek with tents, a cook and porters. Some agency pages now mention basic lodges or shelters at the old campsites while others still pitch tents there. Published heights and walking times for the ridge stages differ as well, so treat the figures below as a guide.

Highlights

  • Five small lakes at about 4,100 m, sacred to Shiva
  • Dawn on Dorje Lakpa and the Jugal peaks from the rim above the lakes
  • Six days on one ridge, from terraced farms to open pasture
  • Rhododendron forest in flower in April
  • A gradual climb that gives your body time to adjust
  • Very few other trekkers outside the August pilgrimage

Challenging: Not technical and never very high, but five nights on a ridge with no villages, a camping crew to organize and an 1,800 m descent in one day make it harder than the altitude suggests.

Ways to change it

The short way from Bhotang

Most people now reach the lakes from the Bhotang side: bus to Chhimti, up to Tupi Danda and Nasim Pati, and on to the lakes on the third day, four or five days in all, with teahouse beds in season. It needs no tents, but it climbs from about 1,800 m to 4,100 m in three days, which is too fast for many people.

Walk out to Melamchi

The classic ending carries on down the valley on foot, from Tupi Danda to the Tamang village of Dhap and then to Melamchi Pul Bazaar for the bus. It makes ten days, and most of the extra walking is now on the valley road.

Ride to Sano Okhareni

A hired jeep along the ridge road from Chautara saves the first walking day and brings the trek down to eight days.

A spare day at the lakes

A second night at Panch Pokhari gives two chances of a clear dawn and time to walk the rim. Take it only if everyone slept well on the first night.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. The lakes and the upper ridge are inside Langtang National Park, and the Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023. Checks on the trail are inconsistent, and we know of no checkpost on this ridge. Here a guide is a practical need in any case: the trail is unmarked and faint across the pastures, mist is common, only people who have walked it know where the water is, and the camping crew has to be arranged through an agency.

Langtang National Park

National park

Langtang National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: At the park checkpoint in Dhunche on the drive to Syabrubesi, or in advance at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

The Nepal Tourism Board and current guides agree on NPR 3,000 and NPR 1,500. Some agencies add 13% VAT to the foreigner fee (NPR 3,390); we could not confirm that it is charged, so carry the difference. NPR 100 for Nepali citizens is the standard mountain-park rate; the tourism board's page still shows an older figure.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.

Getting there

Chautara is about 95 km from Kathmandu by the Araniko Highway and a side road from Dolalghat, four to six hours by bus or hired vehicle. A dirt road carries on along the ridge through Syaule to Sano Okhareni, and some groups ride that far. The trek ends at Chhimti, above Bhotang, where the bus to Kathmandu starts; the road down the Indrawati valley to Melamchi is rough and can close in the monsoon.

  • Bus: Kathmandu to Chautara · 4–6 h

    Public buses run through the day; ask your hotel the day before which bus park they leave from. A hired jeep is quicker and can carry the camping gear.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Sano Okhareni · 5–6 h

    A hired four-wheel drive can continue along the ridge road beyond Syaule, which saves the first walking day. The public bus is reported to take seven to eight hours.

  • Bus: Chhimti to Kathmandu · 5–7 h

    Down the valley through Melamchi to Jorpati on the edge of Kathmandu. Reports describe an early-morning departure in each direction, so ask at your lodge the night before, or book a jeep.

Hazards on this route

Remoteness on the ridge

Between Sano Okhareni and Nasim Pati there are no villages, no shops and little or no phone signal for four days. Help means a helicopter in clear weather or being carried down to the road. Carry a full first-aid kit, leave your plan with your agency and check that your insurance covers helicopter rescue.

Altitude at the lakes

You sleep at about 3,700 m and then 4,100 m. The ridge route gains height gently, but people still fall ill, most often those who come up fast from Bhotang. Do not leave Nasim Pati with a headache that has not cleared, and take the Bhotang trail down at once if anyone gets worse.

Mist and a faint trail

Cloud builds on the ridge most afternoons and can fill the pastures within minutes. Above Pauwa Bas the path is faint, splits into herders' tracks and is not marked. Keep the group together, start early and stop if you cannot see the way.

Snow out of season

Snow can fall above Hille Bhanjyang in any month from October to May and lies deep from December to March. It hides the trail and turns the rocky ground below the lakes into a slow, slippery climb. Turn back if fresh snow covers the route.

The long descent

The drop from the lakes to Tupi Danda is about 1,800 m in one day and steep for most of it. Use two poles and take it slowly. If your knees are doubtful, split it with a second night at Nasim Pati.

Practical tips

  • Buy the Langtang National Park permit and the TIMS card in Kathmandu before you leave. We know of no counter on this side of the park.
  • Agree with your agency in writing what the crew will carry: tents, a kitchen, fuel and food for at least five days on the ridge.
  • Carry at least two litres of water each from every camp and treat all of it. Ask your guide each morning where the next source is.
  • Bring a four-season sleeping bag. Nights at Nasim Pati and the lakes are below freezing even in April and October.
  • Avoid the week of Janai Purnima in August unless you are going as a pilgrim. The shelters overflow and the trail is deep mud.
  • Draw all your cash in Kathmandu, and carry small notes for the teahouses at Nasim Pati and Tupi Danda.

We know of no cash machines on this route. Carry all the rupees you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Panch Pokhari trek take?

Most people take 8–10 days. The itinerary on this page is 9 days.

How high does the Panch Pokhari trek go?
