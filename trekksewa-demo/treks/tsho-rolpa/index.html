Tsho Rolpa & Rolwaling Valley

A ten-day walk from the Tamakoshi road up the Rolwaling valley, through Simigaon and Beding to the summer village of Na and the glacial lake of Tsho Rolpa at 4,580 m.

Is this trek right for me?

  • The walking

    10 days in all. Most walking days are 4 to 6 hours on foot, and the longest is about 8 hours. Long days and several nights above 4,000 m, or a pass above 5,000 m.

  • The height

    You sleep as high as 4,180 m. Most people feel the altitude at this height. The plan includes the rest days you need: do not shorten it.

  • Where you sleep

    In lodges every night: a simple twin room, a shared bathroom and hot meals in a warm dining room.

  • The help you need

    This is a restricted area. A registered trekking company must arrange your permit, and a licensed guide walks with you.

  • What it costs

    A guided trip with a trekking company usually costs USD 600 to 1,000 a person, going by the prices 10 companies published for 2026.

Overview

Rolwaling runs east from the Tamakoshi river toward the back of Khumbu: a narrow valley walled in by Gaurishankar (7,134 m) and a line of peaks above 6,000 m. A small Sherpa community lives here, moving with the seasons between winter fields low down, the main village of Beding and the summer settlement of Na. The valley is held to be a beyul, one of the hidden valleys of Tibetan Buddhist tradition, and by local custom no animal is killed in it.

At the head of the valley, behind a wall of moraine, lies Tsho Rolpa, a lake about three and a half kilometres long that has grown as the Trakarding Glacier has shrunk. In 2000 engineers cut a channel through the moraine to lower the water by three metres, and the lake is still one of the most closely watched in the Himalaya because of the flood risk to villages downstream. For a trekker it is a stark place: grey-green water, ice at the far end and hardly anyone else there.

Next to Khumbu this is a simple trek. Every stage has lodges, but they are family houses with a few beds, the food is dal bhat, and beyond the road there is no clinic and very little phone signal. Rolwaling is a restricted area, so the permit has to be arranged through a registered agency and you walk with a licensed guide. Almost everyone goes in and out the same way: the only exit at the top is the Tashi Lapcha, a glacier pass for roped parties.

Highlights

  • Tsho Rolpa, a glacial lake three and a half kilometres long at 4,580 m
  • Gaurishankar (7,134 m) above Simigaon and Beding
  • Beding and Na, the villages of the Rolwaling Sherpas
  • The forested gorge of the Rolwaling Khola
  • A valley with only a handful of trekkers, even in peak season

Challenging: Steep, rough trails, basic lodges, a jump of almost 900 m in sleeping height to Beding and no quick way out make it harder than its length suggests.

Ways to change it

Eight or nine days

Climb to Simigaon on the evening of the drive, and walk from Na down to Beding on the afternoon after the lake. This suits fit parties who are already acclimatized; do not drop the rest day at Beding.

Toward Yalung Ri base camp

With an extra night at Na, a day walk climbs south into the Yalung valley toward the base camp of Yalung Ri, at around 4,900 m, for a view back across the valley to Gaurishankar.

Over the Tashi Lapcha to Khumbu

The 5,755 m pass beyond the lake leads to Thame in four to five days. It needs ropes, crampons, tents for nights on the glacier and a crew with climbing experience. It is a mountaineering expedition, not an extension of this trek.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. Rolwaling is a restricted area: the permit is issued only through a registered trekking agency, and you must walk with a licensed guide. Since March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker, so you no longer need a second person in order to apply. A guide who knows the valley matters in practice too: they phone ahead to find out which lodges in Dongang, Beding and Na are open, which decides whether the trip is possible at all outside the main seasons.

Gaurishankar CA

Conservation area

Gaurishankar Conservation Area Permit · National Trust for Nature Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, single entry

Where: Online at NTNC's e-permit site or at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu. A permit issued at a trail checkpost costs double.

The fee includes tax and children under 10 do not need a permit. NTNC lists Rolwaling and Tsho Rolpa, Kalinchowk and the Shivalaya walk-in towards Everest under this permit. Upper Rolwaling needs the Dolakha restricted-area permit too.

Rolwaling RAP

Restricted area

Dolakha (Gaurishankar and Lamabagar) restricted area permit · Department of Immigration

All foreign nationalsUSD 20person per week

Where: Department of Immigration in Kathmandu, through a registered trekking agency only.

Since 22 March 2026 the permit can be issued to a single trekker; before that the minimum was two. You still have to book through a registered agency and walk with a licensed guide, with at most seven trekkers per guide. The fee table defines the area as Gaurishankar Rural Municipality ward 9 and Bigu ward 1. The Gaurishankar conservation area permit is extra.

Agency required

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

A long day by road from Kathmandu: the Araniko Highway to the Sun Koshi, over the hills to Charikot, down to the Tamakoshi and north on the hydropower road through Singati to Chetchet. The section beyond Singati is rough and can close after heavy rain.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Chetchet · 8–9 h

    A hired jeep goes straight through and is the only way to arrive with daylight to spare.

  • Bus: Kathmandu to Charikot · 5–6 h

    Regular buses from the old bus park at Ratna Park. Sleep in Charikot or change at once for Singati.

  • Jeep: Charikot to Chetchet · 4–5 h

    Local bus to Singati, then a shared jeep up the valley. Departures thin out after midday.

Hazards on this route

Fast height gain to Beding

Dongang to Beding raises your sleeping height by almost 900 m in a day, with no good place to split it. Take the rest day at Beding seriously. If anyone becomes unwell at Na, go back down to Beding; do not wait there.

Remoteness

Beyond Chetchet there is no road, no staffed clinic and little phone coverage. Evacuation means a helicopter in clear weather or being carried for days. Carry a first-aid kit, leave your schedule with your agency and check that your insurance covers helicopter rescue.

Landslides on the road and lower trail

The Tamakoshi road and the forest trail below Dongang are cut by landslides in the monsoon, and after heavy rain at other times of year. Ask in Charikot or Singati whether the road is open before you set out.

The moraine at Tsho Rolpa

The lake is held back by loose moraine with ice inside it. Keep to the worn path on the crest, stay away from the outlet channel and the steep inner banks, and do not linger on the valley floor directly below the dam.

Closed lodges

Outside the main spring and autumn months the lodges at Na, and sometimes at Dongang, are shut, and Beding empties in midwinter. Without a confirmed bed and meals you need a tent, a stove and food for the upper valley.

Practical tips

  • Bring all your cash from Kathmandu or Charikot. Nothing in the valley takes cards.
  • Carry a warm sleeping bag; lodge bedding is thin and sometimes shared.
  • Meals are vegetarian: by local custom no animal is killed in Rolwaling, so do not ask for meat.
  • Phone signal is patchy beyond Simigaon and often absent above Dongang. Agree check-in days with someone at home before you leave.
  • The restricted area permit is issued in Kathmandu against your passport and visa, so allow a working day there before you set off.
  • Lodge openings change from year to year, and heights quoted for Simigaon and Dongang differ by up to 100 m between maps. Have your guide confirm each night's stop by phone before you leave the road.

We know of no cash machines on this route. Carry all the rupees you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Tsho Rolpa & Rolwaling Valley trek take?

Most people take 8–13 days. The itinerary on this page is 10 days, including 1 day for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Tsho Rolpa & Rolwaling Valley trek go?

The highest point is Tsho Rolpa at 4,580 m (15,026 ft). The highest place you sleep is Na at 4,180 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it challenging. Steep, rough trails, basic lodges, a jump of almost 900 m in sleeping height to Beding and no quick way out make it harder than its length suggests.

Which permits do I need?

Gaurishankar CA, Rolwaling RAP. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?
