Dhaulagiri Circuit

A two-week camping route up the Myagdi Khola to Dhaulagiri Base Camp, then over French Pass and Dhampus Pass to Marpha, with glacier travel and three nights above 4,700 m. Not a teahouse trek.

Is this trek right for me?

  • The walking

    14 days in all. Most walking days are 5 to 6 hours on foot, and the longest is about 9 hours. Remote, long and high, often with camping, snow or glacier travel.

  • The height

    You sleep as high as 5,050 m. That is very high. Only go if you have time for every rest day in the plan, and talk to your doctor first.

  • Where you sleep

    In tents. A crew carries the camp and cooks; there are no lodges on much of this route.

  • The help you need

    The rules require a licensed guide on this route. A porter to carry your bag is your choice.

  • What it costs

    A guided trip with a trekking company usually costs USD 2,000 to 3,100 a person, going by the prices 10 companies published for 2026.

Overview

The Dhaulagiri Circuit is the hardest of the standard treks in central Nepal. It climbs the Myagdi Khola from terraced Magar villages into uninhabited forest, follows the rubble-covered Chhonbardan Glacier beneath the west and north faces of Dhaulagiri I (8,167 m) to base camp, then crosses French Pass (5,360 m) into Hidden Valley and Dhampus Pass (5,240 m) before dropping to the Kali Gandaki at Marpha.

This is not a teahouse trek. Simple homestays exist as far as Boghara, but for the nine nights beyond that there is nothing to rely on, at most a seasonal tea shop in the forest: you need tents, fuel, food, a kitchen crew and porters equipped for snow. The route crosses moving glacier, avalanche and rockfall zones and two high passes, and the camp in Hidden Valley is above 5,000 m with no quick way down. Crews carry rope, ice axes and crampons, and you should already know how to walk in crampons.

Go only with an agency and a guide who have done the route recently, allow spare days for weather and be honest about your experience. Previous trekking above 5,000 m, good fitness and the patience to turn back are the real requirements. In return you get two weeks in big, empty country: until you reach the Jomsom road you may meet only a handful of other parties.

Highlights

  • Camping beneath the north face of Dhaulagiri I at base camp
  • Walking the Chhonbardan Glacier between Dhaulagiri and Tsaurabong Peak
  • Crossing French Pass into the empty expanse of Hidden Valley
  • The long high traverse from Dhampus Pass above the Kali Gandaki
  • Magar villages and wild forest in the Myagdi Khola valley
  • Finishing among the apple orchards of Marpha

Strenuous: A fully supported camping route with glacier travel, rockfall and avalanche exposure, two passes above 5,200 m and a camp above 5,000 m with no easy retreat.

Ways to change it

Dhampus Peak

From a high camp near Dhampus Pass, parties with a climbing permit and mountaineering experience can climb Dhampus (Thapa) Peak, 6,012 m, in a long day. Add one or two days.

Extra night in Hidden Valley

A second night at about 5,000 m is used for Dhampus Peak or simply to wait for weather. It is too high to be restful, so do not add it as a comfort day.

Base camp and back

If the passes are closed or the group is not ready for them, returning from Dhaulagiri Base Camp the way you came is a worthwhile trek in itself and takes about two weeks from Pokhara.

Start higher up the road

When the jeep track beyond Darbang is open, some crews drive as far as Takam or Muri and save a day, at the cost of a faster start for your legs and lungs.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, though checks on the trail are inconsistent. On this route the rule is beside the point: without a full camping crew and a guide who knows the glacier, the timing of the rockfall above Italian Base Camp and the line across the Dhampus traverse in cloud, you should not be here.

ACAP

Conservation area

Annapurna Conservation Area Permit · National Trust for Nature Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, single entry

Where: Online at NTNC's e-permit site, or in person at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu, and the Nepal Tourism Board office in Pokhara. A permit issued at a trail checkpost costs double.

The fee includes tax and children under 10 do not need a permit. Paying online by card adds a 2.9% gateway charge. Covers every trek in the Annapurna range and Lower Mustang; Upper Mustang and Nar Phu need a restricted-area permit as well.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.

Getting there

Drive from Pokhara to Beni and on up the Myagdi Khola to Darbang, five to six hours in all. A rough track continues towards Takam and Muri, and some crews start walking higher up when it is open. The trek ends at Marpha or Jomsom, with a morning flight or a day's drive back to Pokhara.

  • Jeep: Pokhara to Darbang · 5–6 h

    Private jeep via Beni, the usual choice for a crew with camping loads.

  • Bus: Pokhara to Beni · 3–4 h

    Frequent buses. Change at Beni for a local jeep or bus to Darbang, another one and a half to two hours.

  • Flight: Jomsom to Pokhara · 20–25 min

    Morning only, and often cancelled by wind or cloud.

  • Jeep: Jomsom to Pokhara · 7–9 h

    The fallback when flights are grounded, down the Kali Gandaki road via Tatopani and Beni.

Hazards on this route

Altitude with no quick descent

From Dhaulagiri Base Camp to the far side of Dhampus Pass you are above 4,700 m for three nights, and Hidden Valley can only be left over a pass. Helicopters cannot always reach it. Acclimatize properly lower down, make sure the crew carries altitude medication and turn back from base camp if anyone is unwell.

Rockfall and avalanche above Italian Base Camp

The gorge between Italian Base Camp and Glacier Camp is swept by stones and by avalanches from both walls, more so in the afternoon and after snowfall. Cross early, keep gaps between people, do not stop in the gullies and wear a helmet if you have one.

Glacier travel

Most of the Chhonbardan Glacier is covered in rubble, but there are crevasses and unstable ice near base camp and on the way to French Pass, hidden after fresh snow. Follow the guide's line exactly and rope up where the crew asks.

Weather on the passes

Storms bring whiteout and deep snow to French Pass, Hidden Valley and the Dhampus traverse with little warning. Parties have been pinned down for days, and frostbite is a real risk for trekkers and porters alike. Carry spare food and fuel and keep buffer days.

Exposed trail in the lower valley

Between Muri and Dobang the path crosses steep grass, landslide scars and rock steps high above the river, with log bridges over side streams. It is slippery when wet. Do not underestimate it because it is low.

Practical tips

  • Choose the agency for its record on this route: ask when their guide last crossed both passes and what rescue and communication equipment the crew carries.
  • Bring crampons or solid microspikes, a four-season sleeping bag rated to minus 20 °C and a down jacket, plus an ice axe if you know how to use one.
  • Check that your insurance covers helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 m, and that the porters are insured and equipped for snow.
  • Build in at least two spare days for weather. Tight schedules push groups over the passes in conditions they should wait out.
  • There is no phone signal for most of the route beyond Muri; a satellite phone or messenger is the only link.
  • Camp names and heights vary between operators, especially Sallaghari, Glacier Camp and Hidden Valley, and the road head keeps moving up the valley. Compare itineraries by the height gained each day, not by place name.

Cash machines on the route: Jomsom. They run out of cash and go offline, so carry what you need from Pokhara. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Dhaulagiri Circuit trek take?

Most people take 13–18 days. The itinerary on this page is 14 days, including 2 days for rest and acclimatization.

How high does the Dhaulagiri Circuit trek go?

The highest point is French Pass at 5,360 m (17,585 ft). The highest place you sleep is Hidden Valley at 5,050 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it strenuous. A fully supported camping route with glacier travel, rockfall and avalanche exposure, two passes above 5,200 m and a camp above 5,000 m with no easy retreat.

Which permits do I need?

ACAP, TIMS card. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.
