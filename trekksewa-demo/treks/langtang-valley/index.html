Langtang Valley

The Langtang Khola trail from Syabrubesi through forest and yak pasture to Kyanjin Gompa at 3,870 m, with a day hike to Kyanjin Ri or Tserko Ri before you walk back out.

Langtang: road to Syabrubesi cut by the August 2026 flood

The Bhote Koshi flood of 26 August 2026 badly damaged Syabrubesi and the road to it. Reports in mid-November 2026 said vehicles were stopping at Dhunche or Thulo Syabru, with the Langtang valley and Gosaikunda trails open again from there. The Tamang Heritage Trail runs through the worst-hit area and its status was unclear.

What to do: Confirm the current roadhead and which bridges are in use with your agency or a lodge owner before you book transport.

Overview

Langtang is the nearest of Nepal's high valleys to Kathmandu, and you reach it entirely by road. The trail follows the Langtang Khola east from Syabrubesi: two days in a steep, forested gorge, then a wide glacial valley of yak pastures, mani walls and stone villages that ends at the lodges of Kyanjin Gompa, under Langtang Lirung.

The walking is straightforward and there is no pass to cross, but the valley climbs quickly. The usual plan puts you at 3,430 m on the second walking night and 3,870 m on the third, so expect to feel the height at Kyanjin Gompa. The second night there is not a luxury: it is what makes the climb to Kyanjin Ri at 4,773 m, or the much longer day on Tserko Ri at 4,984 m, a pleasure rather than a struggle. You return down the same valley.

The valley has been hit twice. The earthquake of 25 April 2015 sent an avalanche and landslide over Langtang village, killing more than 240 residents, trekkers and trekking staff; the village and most lodges on the trail have been rebuilt by the families who survived. Then on 26 August 2026 a devastating flood on the Bhote Koshi badly damaged Syabrubesi and cut the road to it. The valley trail was not in the flood's path and was reported open again that October by way of Dhunche and Thulo Syabru.

Highlights

  • Two days from oak and bamboo gorge to open glacial valley
  • Kyanjin Gompa, ringed by Langtang Lirung, Kimshung and Naya Kanga
  • Sunrise from Kyanjin Ri, or a full day on Tserko Ri
  • Fresh yak cheese from the Kyanjin factory, started in the 1950s
  • Mani walls, prayer wheels and Tibetan-influenced village life

Moderate: A good trail with no pass, but it climbs about 2,400 m in three walking days and the day hike from Kyanjin Gompa tops out near 4,800 m.

Ways to change it

Start from Dhunche and Thulo Syabru

The approach in use since the 2026 flood. From Dhunche the old trail runs through Thulo Bharkhu and Brabal to Thulo Syabru, then drops to the Langtang Khola and joins the main route below Bamboo. On foot from Dhunche it adds a day each way; a jeep to Thulo Syabru, when the track is open, saves it.

Tserko Ri as an extra day

A third night at Kyanjin Gompa lets you climb Kyanjin Ri first and Tserko Ri the next morning, which is kinder on your body than going straight for the higher top.

Langshisha Kharka

A long, nearly level day east along the valley floor to the yak pastures at its head and back, eight to nine hours with no lodges on the way.

High trail through Sherpagaon

Between Syabrubesi and Rimche a higher path through Khangjim and Sherpagaon stays in the sun and avoids the gorge. It is a little longer and a good way to vary the return.

Continue to Gosaikunda

Instead of walking out to the road, climb from below Bamboo to Thulo Syabru and on to Chandanbari and the Gosaikunda lakes. Allow four more days to Dhunche. You arrive at the lakes already acclimatized.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, though checks on the trail are inconsistent. Here a guide earns the fee twice over: by knowing which roadhead, bridges and lodges are in use after the 2026 flood, and by judging the weather and your condition on the Kyanjin Ri or Tserko Ri day.

Langtang National Park

National park

Langtang National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: At the park checkpoint in Dhunche on the drive to Syabrubesi, or in advance at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

The Nepal Tourism Board and current guides agree on NPR 3,000 and NPR 1,500. Some agencies add 13% VAT to the foreigner fee (NPR 3,390); we could not confirm that it is charged, so carry the difference. NPR 100 for Nepali citizens is the standard mountain-park rate; the tourism board's page still shows an older figure.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.

Fees and rules change, sometimes mid-season. Treat these as a planning guide and confirm with the Nepal Tourism Board, the Department of Immigration or a registered agency before you travel. Last reviewed 2026-11. All permits and fees

Getting there

Road only. Buses and jeeps leave Machhapokhari in Kathmandu early in the morning for Dhunche and Syabrubesi, seven to nine hours away on the Pasang Lhamu Highway. The Bhote Koshi flood of August 2026 cut the last section: as of November 2026 vehicles stop at Dhunche or Thulo Syabru and trekkers join the valley trail near Bamboo. Confirm the working roadhead before you book.

  • Bus: Kathmandu to Syabrubesi · 7–9 h

    The classic approach, by local or express bus from Machhapokhari. Closed short of Syabrubesi since the August 2026 flood; check whether it has reopened.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Syabrubesi · 6–7 h

    Shared or private four-wheel drive. Faster and less cramped than the bus, with the same caveat about the road.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Dhunche · 5–7 h

    The working roadhead since the flood, reported open to jeeps and light vehicles. A rough track continues from Dhunche to Thulo Syabru when conditions allow.

Hazards on this route

Road and roadhead after the 2026 flood

The Bhote Koshi flood of 26 August 2026 destroyed bridges and long stretches of road around Syabrubesi. The valley trail lies on a different river and reopened in late October 2026 by way of Dhunche and Thulo Syabru. Check which approach is open in the week you travel, and expect the first and last stages to differ from the classic plan.

Altitude

The standard plan goes from 1,460 m to 3,870 m in three walking days, faster than the usual guideline. Mild headaches at Langtang village and Kyanjin Gompa are common. Do not go higher with symptoms, and do not try Kyanjin Ri or Tserko Ri if you felt unwell overnight.

Landslides and rockfall

The gorge below Lama Hotel and the slopes between Ghodatabela and Langtang village shed rock, especially during and just after the monsoon. Do not linger in slide paths, and ask lodge owners about recent falls.

Weather on the high day

Kyanjin Ri and Tserko Ri are exposed ridges. Wind and cloud build quickly after midday, and the paths near the tops are faint under snow. Start early, carry a warm layer and a head torch, and turn back if you lose the view.

Practical tips

  • Bring enough rupees from Kathmandu for the whole trek. Do not count on finding a working ATM after Trishuli Bazaar.
  • Spend two nights at Kyanjin Gompa. With only one you either skip the day hike or walk it tired.
  • Lodges at Lama Hotel fill by mid-afternoon in October and November; Rimche, twenty minutes lower, usually has room.
  • Treat all water. Lodges sell boiled or filtered water, which saves buying plastic bottles.
  • Walk the path across the old village site quietly. Many of those who died in 2015 were never recovered, and their families run the lodges you sleep in.
  • Mobile coverage is patchy in the gorge; lodges from Langtang village upward sell Wi-Fi by the day.

We know of no cash machines on this route. Carry all the rupees you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Langtang Valley trek take?

Most people take 7–10 days. The itinerary on this page is 8 days.

How high does the Langtang Valley trek go?

The highest point is Kyanjin Ri at 4,773 m (15,659 ft). The highest place you sleep is Kyanjin Gompa at 3,870 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. A good trail with no pass, but it climbs about 2,400 m in three walking days and the day hike from Kyanjin Gompa tops out near 4,800 m.

Which permits do I need?

Langtang National Park, TIMS card. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, though checks on the trail are inconsistent. Here a guide earns the fee twice over: by knowing which roadhead, bridges and lodges are in use after the 2026 flood, and by judging the weather and your condition on the Kyanjin Ri or Tserko Ri day.

When is the best time to go?

Mar–May, Oct–Nov are best. October and November are the clearest months. March to May is warmer, with rhododendron in the forest below Ghodatabela and some afternoon cloud. December to February is cold and quiet, with snow possible above Langtang village. From June to early September expect rain, leeches in the gorge and landslides on the road.
