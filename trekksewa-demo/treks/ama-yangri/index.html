Ama Yangri

A short trek from the Melamchi valley to the Hyolmo village of Tarkeghyang, then up through rhododendron forest to the 3,771 m summit of Ama Yangri.

Overview

Ama Yangri is the forested mountain that stands over Tarkeghyang, in upper Helambu. The Hyolmo people regard it as a protecting mother, and a stupa and ranks of prayer flags mark its 3,771 m summit. On a clear morning the top gives one of the widest views within easy reach of Kathmandu: the Langtang and Jugal peaks close at hand, and a long line of snow fading away to east and west.

It has become the weekend mountain for hikers from Kathmandu, who often drive to Tarkeghyang, climb before dawn and are home the next night. The three-day version here is kinder: you walk up from Timbu on the first day, which gives your legs and lungs a night at 2,600 m before the summit. Do not underrate the summit day. It is nearly 1,200 m straight up through forest and the same back down, to a height where a visitor fresh from the city can easily get a headache.

Tarkeghyang has comfortable lodges, but there is nothing on the mountain itself, so you carry water and lunch. In January and February the upper trail holds snow, which draws crowds on Saturdays. March and April bring rhododendron bloom for most of the climb, and October to December the clearest skies.

Highlights

  • Sunrise from the summit stupa, with Langtang and Jugal close at hand
  • Rhododendron forest in bloom in March and April
  • Tarkeghyang, the largest Hyolmo village, and its monastery
  • A real mountain summit within a long weekend of Kathmandu

Ways to change it

Two-day version

Take a jeep all the way to Tarkeghyang on the first day, climb at dawn on the second and drive home that afternoon. It is the usual plan for hikers from Kathmandu, and the one most likely to leave you with an altitude headache.

Out through Sermathang

On the third day, walk the near-level trail from Tarkeghyang to Sermathang in three to four hours instead of dropping to Timbu, and leave by jeep from there next morning.

As part of the Helambu circuit

On the Helambu circuit, add a second night at Tarkeghyang and climb Ama Yangri as a day trip. You will be better acclimatized after the night at Tharepati.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, though checks are inconsistent and on this short route, which most Nepali hikers walk unguided, they are rare. A guide is still worth having for the summit day: the forest trail has unmarked forks, there is no water or shelter above the village, and cloud can cover the top within minutes.

Langtang National Park

National park

Langtang National Park entry permit · Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation

Foreign nationalsNPR 3,000person, single entry
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,500person, single entry
Nepali citizensNPR 100person

Where: At the park checkpoint in Dhunche on the drive to Syabrubesi, or in advance at the Tourist Service Centre in Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu.

The Nepal Tourism Board and current guides agree on NPR 3,000 and NPR 1,500. Some agencies add 13% VAT to the foreigner fee (NPR 3,390); we could not confirm that it is charged, so carry the difference. NPR 100 for Nepali citizens is the standard mountain-park rate; the tourism board's page still shows an older figure.

TIMS card

Registration

Trekkers' Information Management System card · Nepal Tourism Board and the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal

Foreign nationalsNPR 2,000person, per trek
SAARC nationalsNPR 1,000person, per trek

Where: Only through a registered trekking agency, which applies in the Nepal Tourism Board's online system with your route and your guide's details. Individual trekkers can no longer buy one over the counter.

Since the revised rules of 31 March 2023 the card is tied to trekking with a licensed guide on the tourism board's listed routes. It is not used in Khumbu, where the municipality's Trek Card took its place in 2017. For restricted areas the published rules pull both ways: TAAN's pages say TIMS applies in every trekking area, restricted ones included, yet also list visitors holding Department of Immigration permits among those who do not need one, and agencies disagree. Your agency, which is compulsory there anyway, will know what the checkposts on your route ask for. Reports from 2025–26 describe patchy checks of the card, especially in Annapurna.

Getting there

Buses and jeeps run from Kathmandu to Timbu, at the head of the Melamchi valley road, in five to six hours. The route passes Melamchi Pul Bazaar and follows the river that flooded in 2021, so it is slow and rough in places. A hired jeep can continue up the dirt road to Tarkeghyang when it is dry.

  • Bus: Kathmandu to Timbu · 5–6 h

    Morning departures. Ask your lodge or agency for the current departure point, which has changed over the years.

  • Jeep: Kathmandu to Tarkeghyang · 5–6 h

    Skips the walk up from Timbu and turns the trip into a two-day outing. The final climb is on a rough track that can close after rain or snow.

Hazards on this route

A fast climb to 3,771 m

Most people on the summit were in Kathmandu the day before. Headache, nausea and unusual tiredness on the upper slopes are altitude, not just effort. Turn back if they build; they ease quickly as you descend.

Snow and ice in winter

From late December to February the stone steps and the open slopes near the top are often under packed snow. The climb is still done, but a slip on the descent is the usual way people get hurt. Carry microspikes and poles, or wait for a thaw.

Cloud and wind on top

The summit is exposed, and more than one path leaves it. In cloud it is easy to start down the wrong one. Keep the group together, note the way you came up, and do not linger if the weather is closing in.

The Melamchi valley road

The road to Timbu was badly damaged by the floods of June 2021 and still suffers washouts and landslides in heavy rain. Check its state before travelling in the monsoon, and allow extra time.

Practical tips

  • Carry at least two litres of water and something to eat for the summit day; there is no tea shop on the mountain.
  • Take a warm jacket, hat and gloves even in spring. You stand still on a windy top at dawn.
  • Saturdays and public holidays bring large groups from Kathmandu. Go midweek for a quiet summit and an easier choice of rooms.
  • The summit is a shrine. Walk round the stupa clockwise and take your rubbish back down with you.
  • Bring cash from Kathmandu; do not count on an ATM once you leave the city.

We know of no cash machines on this route. Carry all the rupees you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Ama Yangri trek take?

Most people take 2–4 days. The itinerary on this page is 3 days.

How high does the Ama Yangri trek go?

The highest point is Ama Yangri at 3,771 m (12,372 ft). The highest place you sleep is Tarkeghyang at 2,600 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it moderate. Only three days, but the summit day climbs and descends almost 1,200 m on steep forest path, with little time to adjust to the height.

Which permits do I need?

Langtang National Park, TIMS card. Fees and where to get each one are listed in the permits section above.

Do I need a guide?

The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, though checks are inconsistent and on this short route, which most Nepali hikers walk unguided, they are rare. A guide is still worth having for the summit day: the forest trail has unmarked forks, there is no water or shelter above the village, and cloud can cover the top within minutes.

When is the best time to go?

Mar–Apr, Oct–Dec are best. October to December has the clearest mornings. March and April are the rhododendron months. January and February bring snow on the upper mountain, which is popular with local hikers but makes the steep sections slippery. In the monsoon the forest is wet and full of leeches, the summit is usually in cloud and the valley road is prone to washouts.
