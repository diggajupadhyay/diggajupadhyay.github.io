Chisapani to Nagarkot

A three-day walk from Sundarijal through Shivapuri forest to Chisapani, along the valley rim to Nagarkot, and down to the temple village of Changu Narayan.

Is this trek right for me?

  • The walking

    3 days in all. Most walking days are 4 to 5 hours on foot, and the longest is about 8 hours. Short days below 3,500 m on good paths. Fine for a first trek.

  • The height

    You never sleep above 2,175 m, so altitude is not a worry on this trek.

  • Where you sleep

    In lodges every night: a simple twin room, a shared bathroom and hot meals in a warm dining room.

  • The help you need

    The rules require a licensed guide on this route. A porter to carry your bag is your choice.

  • What it costs

    A guided trip with a trekking company usually costs USD 190 to 250 a person, going by the prices 10 companies published for 2026.

Overview

This is the standard short trek from Kathmandu, and a good answer to two common questions: what to do with three spare days, and how to test your legs before something bigger. It follows the northern and eastern rim of the valley from Sundarijal to Nagarkot, with a night at each of the two best-known viewpoints, and never goes above 2,420 m.

Do not expect wilderness. The first day is a proper forest climb inside Shivapuri Nagarjun National Park, but from Chisapani on you walk dirt roads, village paths and pine-forest tracks through Tamang farmland, with the city sometimes in view below. What you come for is the dawn. On a clear morning Chisapani looks out on Ganesh Himal, Langtang Lirung and Dorje Lakpa, and Nagarkot adds a longer skyline that reaches Everest on the best days.

The middle day is long, seven to eight hours, and the views depend entirely on the season: sharp from October to March, hazy from April, and mostly cloud in the monsoon. Both overnight stops have hotels from basic to comfortable, so you can walk with a day pack. With a fourth day, continue from Nagarkot to Dhulikhel instead of dropping to Changu Narayan.

Highlights

  • The Changu Narayan temple, a World Heritage monument
  • A trek you can start by taxi after breakfast

Ways to change it

On to Dhulikhel

From Nagarkot, follow the ridge south past the view tower and down through Rohini Bhanjyang and Tanchok to Dhulikhel in five to six hours, then take a bus back to Kathmandu or stay the night.

Namobuddha and Panauti

From Dhulikhel a further day's walk leads to the stupa and monastery at Namobuddha and down to the old Newar town of Panauti, where buses leave for Kathmandu. With this the trek becomes five days.

Two-day version

Skip the night at Nagarkot: walk from Chisapani to Nagarkot and take a late-afternoon bus or taxi back to Kathmandu. It works if you start at dawn, but you lose the Nagarkot sunrise.

Permits and guide rules

Guide required. The Nepal Tourism Board has required foreign trekkers to hire a licensed guide since April 2023, and checks are inconsistent; on a low route like this one the rule is rarely applied on the trail. The place it matters is the Sundarijal gate, where park staff have at times refused entry to foreigners walking alone. A guide also saves time on the second day, which follows a web of unsigned dirt roads and village paths.

Getting there

Both ends are inside the Kathmandu Valley. Sundarijal is under an hour by taxi from Thamel, or a little longer by local bus. From Changu Narayan, buses and taxis run to Bhaktapur and on to Kathmandu in about an hour and a half. Nagarkot and Dhulikhel also have buses if you finish there.

  • Bus: Kathmandu to Sundarijal · 1–1.5 h

    Local buses from the Ratna Park side of town; a taxi from Thamel takes about 45 minutes.

  • Bus: Changu Narayan to Kathmandu · 1.5–2 h

    Local bus to Bhaktapur, then a frequent bus to Kathmandu. A taxi does it in about an hour.

  • Bus: Nagarkot to Kathmandu · 1.5–2 h

    For those who skip the last day's walk. Most buses run by way of Bhaktapur.

  • Bus: Dhulikhel to Kathmandu · 1.5 h

    Frequent buses along the Araniko Highway, if you take the Dhulikhel extension.

Hazards on this route

Losing the route on day two

Between Chisapani and Nagarkot the route follows jeep tracks that fork often and are rarely signed. A wrong turn can add hours to an already long day. Keep the ridge line in mind, ask at every tea shop and carry an offline map.

Walking alone

There have been attacks on lone hikers in the forests around the valley in the past, which is one reason park staff discourage walking alone. Incidents are rare. Go with a companion or a guide, and do not set out from Sundarijal late in the day.

Monsoon conditions

From June to September the stone steps above Sundarijal are slippery, the forest is full of leeches and the dirt roads turn to mud. Wear shoes with real grip, carry salt or repellent, and expect cloud instead of mountains.

Cold mornings

In December and January both viewpoints are near freezing at dawn and hotel rooms are unheated. Bring a warm jacket, hat and gloves, even though the days are mild.

Practical tips

  • Leave Thamel by 8 am on the first day, so that you reach Chisapani with daylight to spare for the sunset.
  • Carry lunch or snacks on the second day. Tea shops are spread out and may be shut on quiet weekdays.
  • Book Nagarkot ahead for Friday and Saturday nights, when families from Kathmandu fill the hotels.
  • Keep the national park ticket until you are out of the forest beyond Chisapani; it may be checked at the far boundary too.
  • Bring a head torch for the two dawn viewpoints, and small notes for tea shops and buses.

We know of no cash machines on this route. Carry all the rupees you need from Kathmandu. Work out how much.

Quick answers

How long does the Chisapani to Nagarkot trek take?

Most people take 2–5 days. The itinerary on this page is 3 days.

How high does the Chisapani to Nagarkot trek go?

The highest point is Borlang Bhanjyang at 2,420 m (7,940 ft). The highest place you sleep is Nagarkot at 2,175 m.

How difficult is it?

We grade it easy. Low altitude and a hotel bed each night; the only real demands are the first day's climb of nearly 1,000 m and the length of the second day.
